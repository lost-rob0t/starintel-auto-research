* REVIEW Objective

Make Ohio the first state executed from the generated jurisdiction catalog.

The program produces reviewed revisioned source profiles, deployments, search plans, subfamily decisions, gaps, and route assessments for the State of Ohio and all 88 counties. It records authority, source identity, acquisition methods, temporal and jurisdictional scope, refresh behavior, provenance, explicit gaps, applicability, and historical source relationships.

This is a bounded execution design. It does not claim Ohio coverage is complete and does not implement the production runtime.

* REVIEW Architecture Decision

Ohio is one state-scoped run over reusable government-data actors and adapters. Government units and components are durable records and bounded job keys, not permanent actors.

The following durable capabilities must exist before any live wave:

- Government Identity Authority
- Source Profile and Deployment Authority
- Route Assessment Authority or equivalent serialized owner
- immutable Search Plan, Subfamily Decision, and Gap stores
- Raw Artifact Authority and retrieval-event ledger
- Provenance Validator
- Coverage Ledger
- Official Domain Resolver and Platform Fingerprint pools
- Adapter Registry and bounded workers
- Rate and Lease Authority with generation/fencing
- independent review authority

A live wave cannot begin until its exact contract versions, policy snapshot, manifest set, durable authorities, and deterministic replay fixtures are ready.

* REVIEW Parent Protocol Blocker

The current parent =STAR-RESEARCH-PIPELINE-003= protocol is not yet revision-aware enough for this program. Before implementation it must define exact revision fields, route-assessment and review ownership, applicability/policy bindings, lease fencing, and revision-aware routing/checkpoints/results. Ohio review may continue, but implementation is blocked until that parent amendment is reviewed.

* REVIEW Scope

Included:

- State of Ohio and all 88 county GEOIDs
- official and delegated state, county, and component systems
- the full sixteen-route contract in =STAR-GOVDATA-DESIGN-002=
- Franklin County as the first complete pilot
- fingerprint-driven diverse-county validation
- current source identity and immediately visible predecessor, successor, migration, archive, and temporal relationships
- explicit positive, partial, negative, restricted, retired, and not-applicable decisions

Deferred:

- exhaustive municipality, township, school, and special-district coverage
- production crawling and normalization
- automatic records-request sending
- credential creation, terms acceptance, payments, or access-control bypass
- unauthorized protected-data acquisition
- exhaustive historical crawling beyond bounded policy
- continuous scheduling before pilot acceptance

* REVIEW Ohio Identity Set

The initial set contains the State of Ohio, 88 counties, and discovered components such as auditors, recorders, clerks, boards of elections, prosecutors, sheriffs, engineers, treasurers, courts, commissions, health districts, and planning bodies.

Each component records parent, function, authority, service geography, evidence, confidence, and whether the function is county-owned, independently elected, shared, regional, municipal, state-provided, delegated, or not applicable.

* REVIEW Route Contract

All route and subfamily semantics come from one pinned applicability-contract version. The complete sixteen-route inventory is defined in =STAR-GOVDATA-DESIGN-002=, including the operational-metadata cross-cutting route.

State sources may satisfy a county subfamily only when the rule explicitly permits substitution and the source coverage scope proves that county/component/period.

* REVIEW Cross-Wave Historical Identity

Every wave records bounded visible history:

- first/last observed times
- predecessor/successor locators and redirects
- documented migrations and retired-domain evidence
- archive candidates
- earliest/latest visible coverage
- current/prior/future-effective distinctions

Wave 5 expands historical acquisition; it does not introduce historical identity for the first time.

* REVIEW Execution Waves

** Wave 0: Identity, legal baseline, and fingerprint census

1. Load Ohio and all county GEOIDs.
2. Verify state identity and authoritative domains.
3. Capture public-records, open-meetings, retention, and archival authorities.
4. Create unresolved county/component candidates without promotion.
5. Collect bounded platform, vendor, delegation, shared-service, court, and health fingerprints.
6. Record minimal historical identity.

Exit gate:

- state identity is verified
- all 88 county records have stable GEOIDs
- legal/policy sources have hashes and effective dates
- a platform/organizational variation matrix exists
- every observed locator has minimal history
- no county is marked complete

** Wave 1: State source systems

Profile Ohio Laws, Legislature, OhioBuys, Ohio Checkbook, OGRIP/OSIP/LBRS, Secretary of State, Supreme Court, Auditor of State, and archival/retention systems.

Exit gate:

- every family has a reviewed profile revision or reviewed blocked/unsupported decision
- methods are ranked and bound to adapter versions/manifest hashes
- rate, pagination, file, browser, auth, scope, and artifact policies are recorded
- every promoted revision has raw evidence
- county-indexed discovery crosswalks exist where exposed
- Checkbook is not used as a finance-completion decision
- minimal historical identity is recorded

** Wave 2: Franklin County pilot

Franklin County GEOID =39049= is the first complete end-to-end county pilot.

Franklin exit gate:

- all sixteen routes have reviewed route-assessment revisions under one applicability contract and policy snapshot
- every required/triggered subfamily has a reviewed decision revision
- nonterminal states cannot pass
- every not-applicable result has positive authority/service evidence
- every negative result binds a completed search-plan revision and current reviewed gap revision
- exact profile, deployment, decision, search-plan, gap, evidence, artifact, policy, and contract revisions are pinned
- every adapter result pins version, manifest hash, runtime hash, and lease fence
- deterministic replay reproduces the same decision basis and normalized state
- unexplained truncation is zero
- unresolved required manual review is zero

Allowed reviewed terminal states remain contract-dependent: verified, verified-partial, not-published, records-request-required, authentication-required, paywalled, blocked, retired, or not-applicable.

** Wave 3: Fingerprint-driven diverse-county pilot

Initial candidates:

- Cuyahoga =39035=
- Hamilton =39061=
- Lucas =39095=
- Delaware =39041=
- Athens =39009=
- Vinton =39163=
- Hancock =39063=

Final selection maximizes vendor/platform family, hosting authority, publication maturity, independent-office fragmentation, shared services, court/health topology, geography/size, and archive/migration patterns. The final set includes a mid-sized non-large-metro or western/northwestern agricultural case unless evidence proves equivalent coverage elsewhere.

Wave 3 exit gate:

- all selected counties pass Franklin rules
- no county-specific schema fork is required
- selection dimensions are evidence-backed
- reusable platform families cover at least 70% of supported successful endpoints
- one-off adapter proposals include reusable semantics and fixtures
- deterministic replay succeeds for every promoted revision
- unexplained truncation and stale-lease acceptance are zero
- duplicate deployments are reconciled
- manual-review time and unresolved-route rates are measured against a predeclared policy ceiling
- all negative results have current reassessment deadlines

** Wave 4: All 88 counties

Use bounded batches of at most eight active county runs. Batch order is a versioned policy and must respect host, vendor, deployment, tenant, key, credential, and browser-pool leases.

A county cannot be complete while a required subfamily is unassessed, candidate-only, ambiguous, stale, missing applicability evidence, bound to mutable IDs, or dependent on unresolved required manual review.

** Wave 5: Historical acquisition and change reconciliation

Expand archive inventories, reconcile predecessor/successor relationships, establish refresh/retirement schedules, define tombstones/replacements, reconcile state/current/prior captures, and acquire historical content within policy bounds.

* REVIEW County Court Scope

County coverage includes county courts, clerks, and publicly exposed local-court systems serving the county or linked from authoritative directories. Each court has its own profile revisions. A linked subprogram may perform work, but the county route cannot complete until linked assessment revisions are reviewed and bound. No statewide local-case API does not imply not-applicable.

* REVIEW Negative-Finding Freshness

| Class | Default maximum age | Typical routes |
|-------+---------------------+----------------|
| high | 30 days | feeds, meetings, procurement, permits, operational metadata |
| medium | 90 days | open data, finance, elections, public safety, public records |
| low | 180 days | identity, GIS, property, courts, archives |
| legal-versioned | next effective date or 180 days | law and legal policy |

A stale result cannot satisfy completion. Reassessment creates new search-plan, gap, decision, and assessment revisions.

* REVIEW Acceptance Criteria

1. All 88 county GEOIDs are in scope.
2. All sixteen routes use one versioned machine-readable applicability contract.
3. Route decisions pin immutable profile, deployment, decision, search-plan, gap, policy, evidence, artifact, adapter, and runtime revisions.
4. Franklin and Wave 3 pass deterministic terminal-state gates.
5. Historical identity is captured in every wave.
6. Checkbook does not imply finance completeness.
7. Court results bind linked local-court assessments.
8. Negative findings expire by route volatility.
9. Adapter success cannot hide truncation, stale leases, or missing artifacts.
10. No permanent actor exists per county.
11. The parent actor protocol is amended before implementation.

* REVIEW Deterministic Fixtures

Required fixtures include all sixteen route definitions, shared/regional providers, legitimate/rejected not-applicable decisions, stale negative results, oversized files, ArcGIS viewer/REST preference, effective-dated law, public-read/login-write procurement, linked court systems, source migration, partial current-year data, browser capture bundles, OCR-required documents, stale lease fences, artifact-before-checkpoint crash recovery, and replay after later source revisions.

* REVIEW Open Decision

- Statewide batch ordering after pilots: geographic, population, risk, platform family, or documented hybrid.

This operational policy does not alter completion semantics.
