* Executive Finding

All seven ideas are technically credible enough to keep. They are not seven separate projects.

The strongest shared direction is an *evidence-aware intelligence graph*: preserve individual claims and their provenance, allow support and contradiction to coexist, attach time to assertions, retain actor capability descriptions, and make derived state reproducible from raw inputs.

The best-established primitives are provenance, capability discovery, temporal assertions, and replay. Corroboration-aware deduplication and graph-rarity ranking are well-supported research directions but need StarIntel-specific scoring rules. Treating unanswered questions as first-class records is the least standardized idea, but it fits open-world knowledge graphs and automated hypothesis generation well enough to justify an experiment.

* Seed Findings

** 1. Contradictions as first-class data — KEEP

Do not force conflicting claims into one overwritten value. W3C PROV already models entities, activities, agents, derivation, attribution, and qualified relationships, while current RDF-star work is explicitly extending RDF to support statements about statements. That gives StarIntel a standards-backed basis for attaching source, confidence, time, support, or contradiction metadata to individual assertions.

Research implication: contradiction should be representable without deleting either claim.

** 2. Unanswered questions as records — EXPERIMENT

Knowledge-graph research commonly treats missing or unknown facts as distinct from known false facts, and recent hypothesis-generation work uses graph structure to drive new candidate questions or hypotheses. The leap from this literature to a durable =question= document is StarIntel-specific, not an established standard.

Research implication: test a small question record containing target claim, known evidence, missing evidence, status, and actor-generated follow-up tasks.

** 3. Duplicate documents as corroboration — KEEP WITH SOURCE-INDEPENDENCE CHECKS

Knowledge-fusion research treats repeated observations from multiple extractors and sources as evidence that must be fused while accounting for source quality and extraction noise. Therefore exact or near-duplicate claims should not automatically disappear semantically even when duplicate payload storage is removed.

The catch is source dependence: ten sites copying one press release are not ten independent confirmations.

Research implication: deduplicate storage separately from evidence counting, and track source lineage before increasing confidence.

** 4. Actors advertising capabilities — KEEP

The FIPA Agent Management standard defines a Directory Facilitator that acts as a yellow-pages service. Agents register descriptions of the services they provide, and other agents can search those descriptions. This is almost exactly the conceptual precedent for StarIntel actor manifests and capability routing.

Research implication: actor capability advertisement is established agent-system practice, not speculative architecture.

** 5. Time as part of assertions — KEEP

Temporal knowledge graphs exist because many facts are valid only during particular periods. Current research explicitly models timestamps alongside entities and relations because static triples lose changing real-world state.

Research implication: employment, ownership, location, membership, contracts, and similar relations should support temporal qualification rather than relying only on document-level timestamps.

** 6. Graph rarity as an interestingness signal — KEEP AS A RANKER, NOT TRUTH

Graph anomaly detection research focuses on rare or structurally unusual nodes, edges, subgraphs, and entire graphs. This supports using structural rarity as one signal for Auto Research prioritization.

Rarity does not imply wrongdoing, importance, or truth. A useful StarIntel ranker would combine rarity with provenance strength, temporal novelty, source independence, and domain-specific rules.

Research implication: treat graph anomaly score as triage priority only.

** 7. Raw-data replay — STRONG KEEP

Event-sourcing architecture stores an append-only history so application state can be reconstructed by replay. W3C PROV similarly emphasizes derivation chains and reproducibility. StarIntel's equivalent is preserving raw source material, normalization inputs, actor versions, transformation records, and deterministic identifiers sufficiently well to regenerate derived documents and graphs.

Research implication: replayability should be an invariant for important derived intelligence, with snapshots allowed only as acceleration rather than the sole source of truth.

* Priority Order

1. Raw-data replay and provenance chain.
2. Claim-level support and contradiction records.
3. Temporal qualification of relations.
4. Capability-advertising actor manifests.
5. Corroboration-aware deduplication.
6. Graph-rarity ranking.
7. First-class unanswered-question records.

This order reflects maturity and foundational value, not implementation approval.

* Research Gaps

- Define when two observations count as independent corroboration rather than copied lineage.
- Decide whether claim qualification is represented as relation documents, statement nodes, RDF-star-like annotations, or another StarIntel-native structure.
- Determine the minimum replay envelope: raw bytes, request metadata, actor version, parser version, normalization version, timestamps, and deterministic IDs.
- Benchmark simple graph-rarity measures before considering graph neural networks.
- Test whether persistent question records actually improve research completion or merely create backlog noise.

* Footnotes and Glossary

- *Corroboration*: Multiple observations supporting the same claim. Corroboration strength depends on source independence and provenance, not raw duplicate count.
- *Qualified assertion*: A claim whose metadata can include provenance, time, confidence, source, support, contradiction, or other context.
- *Temporal knowledge graph*: A graph in which facts or relations are associated with timestamps or validity intervals.
- *Replay*: Regenerating derived state from preserved inputs and transformation history rather than trusting only the latest stored projection.
- *Graph anomaly*: A structurally unusual node, edge, subgraph, or graph. Anomaly is a ranking signal, not a semantic conclusion.
