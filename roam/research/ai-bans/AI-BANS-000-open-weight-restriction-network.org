* Research Question

Who is calling for, designing, implementing, or amplifying bans and restrictions affecting open-weight artificial intelligence,[fn:open-weight] and how are those actors connected across the Trump administration, the federal government, Congress, Fox News, contractors, laboratories, nongovernmental organizations, and industry alliances?

* Analytic Rule

The dataset classifies policy by practical effect instead of accepting the policy title at face value. A nationality-based restriction can constitute a de facto open-weight ban[fn:defacto-ban] when the restricted country supplies most of the competitive downloadable models. Artificial Analysis reported that its ten highest-ranked open-weight models were produced by China-based laboratories. Epoch AI separately reported that nearly all leading Chinese models were open-weight while frontier United States models remained predominantly closed.[fn:artificial-analysis][fn:epoch]

The graph therefore distinguishes:

- a direct ban on possessing, importing, downloading, distributing, or using model weights
- a federal-device ban
- an intelligence-system ban
- a federal-contractor or procurement ban
- a supply-chain exclusion covering software created with a restricted model
- an export, sanctions, or Entity List control
- a mandatory evaluation, trusted-access, or pre-release control
- ordinary risk research without a verified restriction proposal
- documented opposition to restrictions

Every material assertion is represented as an evidence-bearing typed relation.[fn:typed-relation] Attributed allegations remain attributed. Generic artificial-intelligence safety advocacy is not labeled as ban advocacy without a concrete proposed control.

* Dataset State

The initial seed contains 169 StarIntel documents:

- 75 entity documents
- 94 typed relation documents
- one dataset manifest

Depth one is complete for the selected seed set. The target recursion depth is ten, subject to evidence and duplication stop conditions.

* Findings

** Restriction policy is fragmented across mechanisms

There is not one federal statute titled an open-weight ban. The practical restriction network uses several mechanisms that can converge on the same result:

- DeepSeek-specific federal-device legislation
- intelligence-system prohibitions extending to contractor-operated systems
- contractor restrictions reaching the production process
- periodically updated lists of Chinese applications and products
- sanctions and Entity List authority
- chip and technical-service export controls
- software-supply-chain exclusion
- classified model benchmarking and trusted early access
- proposals to prevent public release of high-risk model weights

This fragmentation permits an actor to oppose a nominal blanket ban while supporting controls that remove most useful open-weight models from a federal or regulated environment.

** Congress has moved beyond an application-only restriction

H.R.1121 and S.765 target DeepSeek on executive-agency information technology.[fn:hr1121][fn:s765] S.1638 reaches contractor use while performing or supporting federal contracts.[fn:s1638] Section 6604 of the fiscal year 2026 Intelligence Authorization Act prohibits DeepSeek on intelligence-community systems, including contractor-operated systems.[fn:iaa26] The reported fiscal year 2027 language broadens the covered definition to products and services from listed People's Republic of China entities.[fn:iaa27]

H.R.7121 proposes an Office of Management and Budget-maintained list of covered Chinese applications on federal devices.[fn:hr7121] These list-based mechanisms are structurally capable of expanding from one application to a substantial portion of the open-weight ecosystem.

Josh Hawley's proposed artificial-intelligence decoupling legislation is the broadest direct political proposal in the seed. Fox News reported that it would prohibit imports and exports of artificial-intelligence technology involving China and restrict research and investment.[fn:fox-hawley] Applied to downloadable models, an import prohibition is a direct access ban rather than merely a procurement rule.

** Trump administration policy is internally mixed

Donald Trump signed a June 2, 2026 artificial-intelligence cybersecurity executive order establishing classified benchmarking and voluntary trusted early access for some frontier models. The White House fact sheet explicitly states that the order does not establish mandatory governmental licensing, pre-clearance, or permitting for model publication.[fn:white-house-eo]

Trump also signed National Security Presidential Memorandum 11, which directs national-security agencies to adapt commercial and open-source artificial-intelligence technologies.[fn:nspm11] These are countervailing facts against labeling Trump personally as a verified blanket open-weight-ban advocate.

At the same time, the federal machinery capable of implementing narrower restrictions is distributed across Treasury, Commerce, the Office of Management and Budget, the Department of Defense, the Department of Homeland Security, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, the Office of the National Cyber Director, and the Office of Science and Technology Policy. The dataset records office, appointment, and directive relations without assuming that every officeholder personally supports every possible restriction.

Tom Cotton directly urged Office of Management and Budget Director Russell Vought to prohibit DeepSeek and other Chinese-developed artificial-intelligence tools throughout the federal government.[fn:cotton] Scott Bessent, Howard Lutnick, Sean Cairncross, Michael Kratsios, Pete Hegseth, Tulsi Gabbard, Kristi Noem, Russell Vought, David Sacks, and Susie Wiles are seeded because their offices or reported advisory roles intersect sanctions, export controls, procurement, intelligence policy, cybersecurity, science policy, military adoption, or White House policy selection. Further recursion must establish their individual positions from primary evidence.

** Fox News functions as an amplification network

The Fox News seed includes reporters Robert Schmad, Morgan Phillips, and Jamie Joseph; anchor Bret Baier; and opinion contributor Rebekah Koffler. The relations distinguish employment or publication from policy advocacy.

Fox amplified:

- Booz Allen Hamilton's recommendation to exclude Chinese models and generated code from government and critical-infrastructure supply chains[fn:fox-booz]
- Anthropic's attributed allegations of industrial-scale model distillation by Chinese laboratories[fn:fox-anthropic]
- a twenty-one-state attorneys general letter supporting federal DeepSeek restrictions[fn:fox-ags]
- Josh Hawley's broad artificial-intelligence decoupling proposal[fn:fox-hawley]

Fox publication alone does not prove that every reporter or host personally endorses a ban. The graph uses =amplifies=, =works_for=, =published_opinion_at=, and =appears_on= rather than collapsing these relationships into =supports_policy=.

** Booz Allen supplies the broadest contractor exclusion theory

The Booz Allen report, as described in Fox coverage, tested Chinese models and recommended banning them from government and critical-infrastructure work. It also proposed removing code generated by those models from software supply chains.[fn:fox-booz] That theory reaches beyond direct model use: a federal vendor could be excluded because a restricted model was used upstream during software development.

The next recursion must identify the report authors, testing methodology, federal contracts, customer agencies, public briefings, and any lobbying or advisory relationships.

** Anthropic supplies a targeted control rationale, not a proven blanket-ban demand

Anthropic published allegations that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax conducted industrial-scale unauthorized distillation.[fn:anthropic-distillation] Its policy materials support strong chip controls and anti-distillation defenses.[fn:anthropic-policy] The seed does not encode Anthropic or Dario Amodei as blanket open-weight-ban advocates because the retrieved evidence does not establish that claim.

The network relevance is indirect but substantial: distillation allegations can provide the factual rationale used by lawmakers, agencies, and media outlets to support sanctions, export controls, access restrictions, or procurement exclusions.

** The Center for AI Policy is an explicit nongovernmental restriction advocate

The Center for AI Policy's comment to the National Telecommunications and Information Administration recommends preventing the weights of high-risk models from becoming widely available.[fn:caip] This is the clearest seeded nongovernmental proposal for controlling model-weight publication itself.

The Center for AI Safety, Center for Security and Emerging Technology, RAND Corporation, and Government Accountability Institute are included as recursive seeds. Their presence does not assert agreement with a blanket ban. The next layers must enumerate leadership, staff, funders, advisory boards, testimony, coalition participation, and concrete policy proposals.

** Palantir and the Open Secure AI Alliance form a counter-network

NVIDIA founded the Open Secure AI Alliance with Palantir and other members. The alliance argues that open models, harnesses, and tools are cybersecurity assets.[fn:osai] NVIDIA also described Palantir deployments of open NVIDIA Nemotron models for United States agencies and critical infrastructure inside governed environments.[fn:palantir-nvidia]

The current evidence places Palantir on the open-model deployment side, not among verified blanket-ban advocates. A sharper recursive question remains: whether Palantir supports general availability while favoring controlled, identity-bound, government-approved deployment environments in sensitive systems.

** Earlier federal policy provides a documented counterposition

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration's 2024 report recommended monitoring risks from current open model weights rather than mandating restrictions.[fn:ntia] The dataset represents this as an =opposes_policy= relation against the Center for AI Policy's release-restriction proposal. This prevents the graph from representing federal policy as unanimous.

* Recursive Frontier

Depth two prioritizes:

1. Treasury officials and outside advisers connected to sanctions or Entity List proposals.
2. Commerce and Bureau of Industry and Security officials connected to chip, service, and model-weight export controls.
3. Office of Management and Budget, General Services Administration, Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, and contractor implementation pathways.
4. Office of Science and Technology Policy and federal artificial-intelligence evaluation personnel.
5. Fox editors, producers, programs, recurring guests, and repeated source relationships.
6. Booz Allen report authors, contracts, customer agencies, and policy affiliations.
7. Anthropic policy personnel, advisers, lobbying disclosures, and safety alliances.
8. Center for AI Policy and Center for AI Safety leadership, staff, board members, funders, and testimony.
9. Every founding member and representative of the Open Secure AI Alliance.
10. Sponsors, cosponsors, staff, witnesses, donors, and cited organizations connected to the identified bills.
11. All signatories and coordinating organizations behind the state attorneys general letter.

The machine-readable queue is stored in =datasets/ai_bans/frontier.json=.

* Limitations

- This is a depth-one seed, not a completed ten-level investigation.
- Reported statements are separated from enacted authorities.
- Allegations are attributed and are not independently adjudicated.
- Formal office relations do not establish personal support for a policy.
- Publication by a media employee does not prove personal endorsement.
- The practical-effect classification is an analytic inference and must remain linked to current model-availability evidence.
