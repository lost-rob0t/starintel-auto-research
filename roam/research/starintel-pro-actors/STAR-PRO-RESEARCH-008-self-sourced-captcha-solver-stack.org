* Research Question

How far can StarIntel replace paid CAPTCHA-provider calls with self-hosted actors while preserving exact capability typing, source-session fidelity, bounded compute, explicit authorization, reproducible training data, and downstream acceptance verification?

* Approval Boundary

The project owner approved extending CAPTCHA research on 2026-08-09 and explicitly authorized a security review. This node approves the research basis, the local-first self-sourcing direction, and the security research boundary described here. It does not claim that every third-party challenge can or should be solved locally.

This work remains restricted to operator-owned systems, accessibility workflows, quality assurance, open research benchmarks, synthetic environments, and explicitly authorized automation. It does not create a public arbitrary-target CAPTCHA relay.

* Executive Finding

Paid providers should become fallback capabilities rather than the default architecture.

A useful share of challenge work decomposes into local capabilities that StarIntel already models independently:

1. capture bounded challenge artifacts from the authorized source runtime;
2. classify the exact challenge family, variant, binding, and required result kind;
3. route to one or more local recognition or geometric strategies;
4. calibrate confidence and abstain when evidence is weak;
5. apply the result inside the original authorized browser/session when binding requires it;
6. verify downstream acceptance separately;
7. escalate only unsupported or low-confidence rows to a local operator or separately approved external provider.

The limit matters: “self-sourced” is not a universal bypass engine. Behavioral-risk systems, device-attestation flows, proprietary browser-bound challenges, and unknown integrations remain =unsupported= until an exact authorized capability has evidence.

* Current Benchmark Evidence

Recent research strengthens the case for a synthetic-data-first local stack, while also showing why one universal vision model is the wrong abstraction.

CaptchaMind reports a programmatically generated training benchmark with 16,000 examples across eight CAPTCHA categories, including region and process supervision. Its reported solver results are 82.9% average success on the benchmark and 71.0% on real-world instances. This is evidence that synthetic labels can train practical local CAPTCHA capabilities; it is not evidence of universal vendor compatibility.

Open CaptchaWorld covers 20 interactive CAPTCHA types. Its reported evaluation shows a large gap between current multimodal browser agents and people: the best reported agent reaches 40.0% pass@1 while humans reach 93.3%. This supports a heterogeneous actor design with specialized solvers, bounded interaction schemas, and abstention instead of routing every task to a general multimodal agent.

* Candidate Local Capability Stack

| Capability | Primary local implementation candidate | Result / role | Runtime notes |
|------------+----------------------------------------+---------------+---------------|
| distorted text / text answer | Tesseract + optional PaddleOCR ensemble | =textAnswer= | CPU-friendly baseline; retain exact-answer confidence and abstention |
| multilingual / harder OCR | PaddleOCR | =textAnswer= | Heavier second pass; promote only after measured improvement |
| audio transcription | =whisper.cpp= with Whisper model lineage | =textAnswer= | Fully local CPU/GPU deployment; keep raw audio ephemeral |
| image grid / object selection | specialized local model exported to ONNX Runtime | =selectionSet= / =coordinates= | Exact label/output schema and benchmark required |
| semantic tile matching | specialized classifier or OpenCLIP-style scorer after separate dependency review | =selectionSet= | Use only when confidence is calibrated on the exact task family |
| simple geometric localization | OpenCV | =coordinates= / bounded =interactionPlan= | Deterministic image geometry and preprocessing; not generic puzzle support |
| proof-of-work on owned fixtures | first-party ALTCHA-compatible lab strategy | =proof= | Useful owned-system fixture path, not wildcard proof bypass |
| session-bound application | isolated Playwright browser context worker | application only | Preserve same-session cookies/state without exporting browser material |
| unusual or unknown authorized challenge | local operator review actor | typed result | Human fallback remains local and auditable |

The stack should prefer small deterministic or specialized models over a general vision-language model when the output contract is narrow.

* Synthetic Data as the Core Strategy

Self-sourcing needs a reproducible corpus pipeline, not a folder of mystery screenshots.

Preferred corpus order:

1. programmatically generated synthetic challenges with deterministic ground truth;
2. first-party StarIntel-owned fixtures and test environments;
3. open research benchmarks whose licenses and redistribution terms are recorded;
4. explicitly authorized captured challenge artifacts with bounded retention;
5. live authorized canaries only for final generalization measurement.

Synthetic generators should cover capabilities without cloning a commercial vendor implementation, for example:

- distorted text with controlled font, rotation, warp, occlusion, noise, color, compression, and resolution variation;
- bounded audio digits or words with speaker, speed, noise, clipping, codec, gain, and silence variation;
- image-grid semantic selection from licensed image pools;
- count and reference-matching tasks;
- simple sequence-click fixtures;
- slider/alignment and rotation fixtures;
- synthetic multi-stage fixtures with explicit process annotations.

Every durable sample needs generator version, deterministic seed, asset/license provenance, exact ground truth, renderer fingerprint, and split identity. Train/validation/test splits should isolate seeds, style packs, asset pools, and renderer variants so a model cannot pass by memorizing one template.

Do not make production depend on harvesting proprietary challenge corpora from arbitrary sites. Do not retain target cookies, account identifiers, session material, browser profiles, or solved tokens as training data.

* Vendor Test Keys Are Integration Fixtures

Vendor-provided test keys are useful for safe integration tests but are not training data. hCaptcha, Cloudflare Turnstile, and Google reCAPTCHA document test or dummy credentials for predictable test behavior. StarIntel should use those only to validate its broker, source-runtime application, and downstream verification contracts on test environments.

* Primary Local Components

** Tesseract

Tesseract remains a mature open-source OCR engine and is suitable as the cheap first-pass =textAnswer= strategy when an artifact has already been tightly cropped and normalized.

** PaddleOCR

PaddleOCR remains an active local OCR toolkit with lightweight and multilingual model families. For StarIntel it is a second strategy to benchmark against Tesseract, not an automatic dependency.

** Whisper and whisper.cpp

OpenAI Whisper provides the model lineage and reference implementation. =whisper.cpp= provides a dependency-light C/C++ local inference implementation with CPU operation, quantization, and multiple acceleration paths. It is a strong deploy-time candidate for a bounded local audio actor without requiring a Python inference service.

** ONNX Runtime

ONNX Runtime provides a cross-platform inference layer and execution-provider abstraction. This lets the actor contract remain independent from the framework used for training. Its own documentation warns that untrusted models can consume excessive memory or compute, reinforcing the need for hash pinning, sandboxing, and resource limits.

** OpenCV

OpenCV supplies deterministic image-processing and localization primitives such as template matching, segmentation, contour work, and geometry. These are useful for exact capabilities and preprocessing, not generic CAPTCHA support.

** Playwright

Playwright browser contexts provide isolated browser sessions and explicit cookie/state handling. That fits the requirement that browser-bound results be applied in the source context instead of exporting full cookie jars or browser profiles through normal actor messages.

** ALTCHA

ALTCHA documents a self-hosted proof-of-work CAPTCHA design. It is useful as an owned-system fixture source and as evidence that a challenge path can be fully self-hosted. Treat it as a first-party or explicitly authorized exact capability.

* Language and Runtime Boundary

Keep orchestration, policy, routing, supervision, capability registration, result typing, deadlines, and secret references in the existing Common Lisp actor control plane.

Do not introduce a Python orchestration layer merely because some model-training ecosystems use Python.

Deployment candidates:

- Tesseract through a bounded native worker or C API;
- =whisper.cpp= through a bounded native worker;
- ONNX Runtime through C/C++ or another thin native worker;
- OpenCV in the image worker where appropriate;
- PaddleOCR as an optional isolated worker when measured accuracy justifies the heavier runtime;
- Playwright only in the separately isolated browser/source worker;
- Python remains acceptable for offline training/export experiments and optional isolated ML workers, not as required broker/control-plane infrastructure.

This preserves one actor protocol even when an inference engine changes.

* Strategy Contract

Every local strategy must register an exact capability. A research-level strategy record needs:

- =strategyId= and immutable version/fingerprint;
- issuer, family, variant, and integration predicates;
- accepted artifact kinds;
- required result kind;
- source-session/network binding;
- model/engine fingerprint;
- model license and provenance record;
- preprocessing fingerprint;
- maximum input bytes, pixels, frames, or audio duration;
- CPU, memory, GPU, and wall-clock budget;
- confidence-calibration version;
- fixture-set fingerprint;
- minimum broker policy threshold;
- abstention behavior;
- current state: =verified=, =experimental=, =degraded=, =invalidContract=, =disabled=, or =unsupported=.

A model name never implies a capability. Eligibility starts only after the exact fixture set demonstrates the required typed result.

* Confidence, Ensemble, and Abstention

Self-hosting is useful only if the actor can say “I do not know.”

For each exact capability:

- preserve raw strategy confidence;
- calibrate it against a held-out fixture set;
- use independent-strategy agreement as evidence where useful;
- return =lowConfidence= rather than forcing an answer below policy threshold;
- distinguish recognition success from downstream target acceptance;
- update acceptance telemetry without silently retraining the production model.

A cheap local answer can still be wrong. Cost cannot override confidence, authorization, or result binding.

* Benchmark Plan

Measure per exact capability, not across every CAPTCHA family:

- exact-answer accuracy for text;
- character error rate for OCR diagnostics;
- word error rate for audio diagnostics;
- exact-set accuracy plus precision/recall for selections;
- coordinate error and intersection-over-union where geometry matters;
- success within the allowed number of bounded steps;
- p50/p95/p99 latency;
- peak resident memory;
- CPU/GPU time;
- abstention rate;
- retry rate;
- false-action rate;
- downstream =accepted/rejected/expired/inconclusive= rate on owned or explicitly authorized fixtures;
- local compute cost per accepted result;
- local operator escalation rate.

Promotion to =verified= requires a pinned engine/model, frozen held-out fixtures, calibrated threshold, stable resource bounds, and a bounded owned-system canary.

* Economics

The useful comparison is not “free versus paid.” It is:

=localCostPerAccepted = (amortizedCompute + energy + modelOps + localReviewLabor) / acceptedRuns=

versus external-provider cost per accepted result.

Self-sourcing should win for recurring OCR, audio, image-selection, and geometry capabilities when local accuracy is high. External providers may remain rational for rare exact gaps when policy allows them and building a local model would cost more than expected usage.

The broker therefore needs a preference such as =preferLocal= rather than assuming either “always external” or “external providers can never be used.”

* Security Research Audit

The local direction reduces third-party disclosure but increases local attack surface.

| Threat | Required control |
|--------+------------------|
| arbitrary-target solve relay | authenticated internal actor messages only; target policy and =captcha:solve= capability; no public solve URL |
| cookie/session exfiltration | browser-session refs stay opaque; same-session apply actor reads secrets locally; no raw cookie/token transport in ordinary messages |
| malicious model | allowlisted hashes, immutable derivations, no production auto-download, quarantine and benchmark before promotion |
| image/audio decompression bomb | strict encoded-size, decoded-dimension, frame-count, sample-rate, duration, and wall-clock ceilings |
| model resource exhaustion | cgroup/systemd CPU-memory-GPU quotas plus actor deadline and cancellation |
| actor-message spoofing | authenticated StarIntel actor identity/capability checks and typed message validation |
| capability widening | exact issuer/family/variant/result/binding match; missing fields never widen support |
| sensitive artifact retention | ephemeral secret store, short TTL, bounded handles, redacted telemetry, explicit corpus promotion only |
| unsafe network egress from inference | recognition workers run without general network access; only separately authorized browser/source workers need target network access |
| low-confidence forced answer | calibrated abstention and local operator/provider fallback policy |
| unsafe action generation | multi-step policies emit only family-specific typed actions; source runtime owns browser/UI application |
| training-data leakage | never train on cookies, tokens, account data, browser profiles, or session-bearing artifacts; preserve dataset provenance |

Research security conclusion: the self-sourced direction is APPROVED provided these controls remain mandatory design invariants. Implementation security is not approved until code and deployment tests verify them.

* Explicit Non-Goals

- browser fingerprint fabrication or anti-detection stealth;
- proxy rotation designed to evade target controls;
- brute-force retry loops against third-party services;
- a public arbitrary-URL solving endpoint;
- automatic solving of behavioral or device-attestation systems without an exact authorized contract;
- online self-training from live target sessions;
- persisting raw cookies, tokens, browser profiles, or challenge media beyond bounded operational need;
- treating local model confidence as downstream acceptance;
- copying proprietary vendor implementations or making uncontrolled live vendor challenge harvesting a training dependency.

* Recommended Implementation Order

1. Implement a fake local strategy through the existing broker/run path.
2. Implement =LocalModelRegistryActor= and frozen benchmark/corpus manifests.
3. Add artifact decode ceilings, no-network recognition sandboxing, cancellation, and resource limits before real media is accepted.
4. Implement =LocalOcrActor= with Tesseract and synthetic/owned fixtures.
5. Benchmark optional PaddleOCR only after the Tesseract baseline exists.
6. Implement =LocalAudioActor= with =whisper.cpp=.
7. Implement ONNX Runtime-based =LocalVisionActor= and OpenCV geometry for one synthetic/owned capability at a time.
8. Implement =LocalBrowserApplyActor= for same-session result application and downstream verification.
9. Add bounded local operator fallback.
10. Measure the observed StarIntel challenge matrix before deciding which paid provider adapters, if any, remain enabled.

* Open Questions

- Which exact challenge families dominate current StarIntel crawler interruptions?
- Is Tesseract alone adequate for observed text artifacts, or does PaddleOCR materially improve accepted-result rate?
- Which small ONNX-exportable vision architecture gives the best CPU latency for the deployment hosts?
- Which open benchmark/model licenses are acceptable for redistribution and reproducible deployment?
- Should =LocalVisionActor= split into selection and coordinate actors once fixtures show divergent resource/threshold policies?
- What retention window is actually required to debug failed local recognition without keeping sensitive artifacts too long?
- Which browser worker integration best preserves the source runtime without duplicating authenticated state?
- What is the measured local compute cost per accepted result before retiring any external fallback?

* Footnotes and Glossary

- *Self-sourced solver* — A locally operated StarIntel capability whose inference and primary benchmark path do not require a per-solve external CAPTCHA service.
- *Exact capability* — A capability matched on challenge issuer/family/variant/version, required fields, result kind, binding, execution class, and current validation state.
- *Abstention* — An explicit solver outcome stating that confidence or capability evidence is insufficient; preferable to a guessed answer or action.
- *Synthetic corpus* — Programmatically generated fixtures with deterministic ground truth, provenance, and replay metadata.
- *Model fingerprint* — Immutable content identity for a model/engine build used to tie runtime behavior to benchmark evidence.
- *Same-session application* — Applying a typed result inside the original authorized browser/session when target acceptance is bound to that context.
- *Downstream verification* — Independent observation of whether the target accepted, rejected, expired, replaced, or could not conclusively evaluate a solver result.
