* APPROVED Decision

StarIntel must not name child victims or survivors in the child-abuse research domain.

The Child Identity Firewall[fn:child-identity-firewall] is a hard system boundary, not a display preference. It applies even when a child's identity is already public in a news report, obituary, police release, court filing, fundraiser, social-media post, or government disclosure.

Only publicly documented adult alleged offenders, defendants, or convicted offenders may become identity-resolution targets. A child, a person who was under eighteen at the event, or a person whose age is unresolved may never become a named target in this domain.

* APPROVED Non-Negotiable Invariants

1. No child name, alias, initials, nickname, birth name, or former name is stored in canonical StarIntel documents[fn:canonical-document].
2. No child name is used in a search query, archive query, court query, filename, document identifier, log message, metric label, prompt, embedding, graph label, or exported report.
3. No exact date of birth, home address, school, daycare, hospital room, photograph, biometric identifier, social-media account, telephone number, email address, or family contact information is retained.
4. Children receive random case-local pseudonyms[fn:pseudonym] such as =child-victim-01=. The same child must not receive a stable identifier across separate cases or datasets.
5. Child records cannot be merged across incidents, households, agencies, or source systems.
6. Public exports use coarse age bands and county-or-state geography only unless a more general location is needed to prevent re-identification.
7. Exact age, exact location, or detailed medical facts may exist only transiently when necessary for incident matching and must be generalized before persistence.
8. Raw source text containing child identity is not committed to the repository, emitted to public artifacts, or retained in ordinary logs.
9. Evidence excerpts replace child identifiers with =[CHILD]= before storage.
10. The system does not construct family, school, social, household, friendship, or associate graphs around a child.
11. Adult defendants may be connected to a case-local child pseudonym only through the minimum relation necessary to represent the public criminal case.
12. Minor alleged offenders are treated as children and remain unnamed.
13. Unknown age fails closed[fn:fail-closed]: no person-name resolution occurs until adult status is established by authoritative evidence.
14. A privacy failure blocks publication even when every other evidence and confidence threshold passes.
15. Retraction, correction, and deletion workflows must preserve the prohibition against reintroducing a child identifier.

* APPROVED Target Boundary

The only named identity target is an adult whose public legal role is supported as one of:

- formally charged defendant;
- indicted defendant;
- person who pleaded guilty or no contest;
- convicted offender;
- sentenced offender;
- adult identified in a final public agency finding when no criminal case exists and the source lawfully identifies that adult.

The following remain nonpublic workflow candidates and cannot create a canonical named person solely from that status:

- rumor;
- anonymous accusation;
- social-media allegation;
- family-court accusation;
- child-protective-services referral;
- unsubstantiated or false report;
- unnamed suspect;
- arrest without a confirmed public charge;
- adult whose role is ambiguous;
- person named only by a copied news story with no accessible underlying record.

Legal status must be preserved exactly. The words =alleged=, =charged=, =indicted=, =pleaded=, =convicted=, =acquitted=, =dismissed=, and =sentenced= are not interchangeable.

* APPROVED Data-Minimization Contract

Data minimization[fn:data-minimization] requires the smallest child-related record capable of supporting the adult public-case claim.

A public child record may contain only:

#+begin_src json
{
  "id": "starintel:child-case-local:<case-id>:01",
  "dtype": "person",
  "data": {
    "display_name": "Child victim 01",
    "age_band": "1-4",
    "public_identity_prohibited": true,
    "cross_case_linkage_prohibited": true
  }
}
#+end_src

Allowed age bands:

- =under-1=;
- =1-4=;
- =5-9=;
- =10-13=;
- =14-17=;
- =unknown-child=.

A public event may contain:

- event month or date when essential to distinguish the public case;
- state and county;
- broad abuse or neglect category;
- outcome class such as fatality, near fatality, serious bodily injury, or charged sexual abuse;
- public adult defendant identifiers;
- agency, court, prosecutor, police, or medical-examiner source references;
- redacted evidence locators and hashes.

A public event must not contain:

- a child's exact residential or school location;
- distinctive medical narrative unnecessary to establish the case;
- sibling identities;
- nonoffending caregiver identities;
- photographs or links whose primary purpose is to display the child;
- obituary text;
- fundraiser text;
- quoted statements from a child;
- details of sexual acts;
- details likely to enable identification from a small community unless indispensable to the adult case and independently reviewed.

* APPROVED Search Firewall

Every retrieval request passes through the Child Identity Firewall before execution.

The firewall:

1. detects names, initials, direct identifiers, addresses, schools, and other child-specific terms;
2. removes or replaces them with nonidentifying event attributes;
3. rejects a query when safe rewriting would destroy its purpose;
4. records a privacy rejection event without recording the prohibited value;
5. permits adult-name confirmation only after the adult candidate was discovered from an event-based or official-record search;
6. prohibits reverse searching from a child's identity to family members, household members, schools, social accounts, or adult associates.

Permitted discovery query structure:

#+begin_src text
<month or date> <county or city> child abuse fatality charged
<county> near fatality child abuse prosecutor
<adult candidate name> <court or prosecutor domain> charged child abuse
#+end_src

Prohibited structure:

#+begin_src text
<child name> abuse
<child name> parents
<child name> school
<child name> obituary
<child name> fundraiser
#+end_src

* APPROVED Retrieval and Redaction Boundary

Untrusted source content[fn:untrusted-content] may contain a child's identity. The retrieval worker may hold the raw response only in transient memory or an explicitly authorized restricted artifact store.

Before any downstream actor receives content, the redactor must remove:

- child names and initials;
- exact birth dates;
- addresses and precise coordinates;
- school, daycare, and classroom identifiers;
- child photographs and captions;
- social handles and contact data;
- sibling and nonoffending-child identities;
- medical-record numbers;
- quoted child statements;
- sexually explicit victim details.

The redacted artifact contains:

- source URL;
- publisher;
- publication and retrieval timestamps;
- content hash of the raw response when policy permits hashing;
- hash of the redacted representation;
- redaction categories and counts;
- redacted evidence text;
- source authority and independence classification.

The public repository receives only redacted artifacts and metadata. A raw-content hash must never be used to reconstruct, redistribute, or expose the raw child-identifying content.

* APPROVED Actor Enforcement

The Child Identity Firewall is enforced at five points:

1. **Input gate** — reject or sanitize operator inputs containing child identity.
2. **Query gate** — reject child-name and child-associate searches.
3. **Retrieval gate** — redact source content before extraction.
4. **Document gate** — validate every candidate StarIntel document before persistence.
5. **Export gate** — rescan reports, graph labels, search indexes, webpages, and electronic publications before release.

No actor may claim that an upstream actor already performed the check. Each gate is independently testable and failure at any gate stops the branch.

* APPROVED Validation Rules

The validator rejects:

- any child name or plausible child initials;
- exact birth dates or exact child ages outside restricted transient matching;
- direct child contact information;
- exact child residence, school, or daycare;
- child photographs or image references;
- stable cross-case child identifiers;
- child-name search queries;
- raw source captures in public storage;
- detailed sexual-abuse narratives involving a child;
- unredacted quotations containing child identity;
- graph edges that expand the child's family or social network;
- adult publication when adult age is unresolved;
- adult publication below the required legal-status threshold;
- any export whose privacy scan is incomplete.

Validation is deterministic[fn:deterministic] where possible and supplemented by an independent privacy reviewer for ambiguous indirect identification.

* APPROVED Fixtures

Required privacy fixtures:

1. news story naming a deceased child and charged adult;
2. prosecutor release using child initials;
3. court document containing exact birth date;
4. police report naming a school and home address;
5. obituary naming siblings and parents;
6. fundraiser containing photographs and medical details;
7. article quoting a surviving child;
8. copied articles repeating the same child name;
9. adult defendant with the same surname as the child;
10. adult whose age is unknown;
11. minor alleged offender;
12. child name embedded in a URL slug;
13. child name embedded in image alt text;
14. child name in a PDF attachment title;
15. child name accidentally produced by a language model;
16. cross-case records that appear to describe the same child;
17. export index containing a prohibited token;
18. log and error paths containing prohibited input.

Every fixture must prove that the adult public case remains researchable while the child identity is absent from stored, indexed, and exported output.

* APPROVED Metrics and Release Gate

Every release reports:

- child-identifier leakage count;
- rejected unsafe queries;
- redacted identifier count by category;
- adult legal-status accuracy;
- adult role-confusion rate;
- false adult identity publications;
- privacy-review disagreements;
- raw-artifact retention count;
- cross-case child-link attempts;
- export-scan coverage.

Release requirements:

- zero known child-identity leaks;
- zero child-name queries;
- zero stable cross-case child identifiers;
- zero public raw source artifacts containing child identity;
- zero named minor alleged offenders;
- complete export scan;
- independent approval of every privacy exception, with the default being no exception.

There is no acceptable leakage rate above zero.

* APPROVED Completion and Supersession

This policy supersedes any future design language that might otherwise permit child names because they are already public. A later policy may strengthen the protections but may not weaken them without an explicit new approval from the user and a dedicated privacy review.

Any implementation that cannot enforce this policy is rejected rather than partially deployed.

* Footnotes and Glossary

[fn:child-identity-firewall] Child Identity Firewall: A set of mandatory checks that prevents a child's identity or identifying details from entering searches, stored documents, graphs, logs, or public exports.

[fn:canonical-document] Canonical StarIntel document: A durable normalized record accepted into the main StarIntel dataset and used by search, graphs, exports, and later analysis.

[fn:pseudonym] Pseudonym: A deliberately nonidentifying label used in place of a real name. A case-local pseudonym is unique only inside one case and cannot be used to connect records across cases.

[fn:fail-closed] Fail closed: Stop or reject the operation when required evidence or a privacy check is missing, rather than continuing under an unsafe assumption.

[fn:data-minimization] Data minimization: Collecting and retaining only the smallest amount of information needed for the stated purpose.

[fn:untrusted-content] Untrusted source content: Text, images, files, or metadata retrieved from outside the system that may be inaccurate, malicious, identifying, or otherwise unsafe and must be validated before use.

[fn:deterministic] Deterministic validation: A check that produces the same result from the same input and rules, without relying on model judgment or random behavior.
