* Normative Scope

This clarification is part of =STAR-RESEARCH-PIPELINE-004=. It does not create a second government-data envelope protocol.

The envelope remains =star.government-data.v1.1=. This document replaces contradictory delivery, identity, applicability, and operational-metadata passages in =PIPELINE-003=, =PIPELINE-004=, =STAR-GOVDATA-DESIGN-002=, =STAR-GOVDATA-DESIGN-003=, =STAR-GOVDATA-DESIGN-004=, and =STAR-LANG-002=.

Approval and implementation treat =PIPELINE-003=, =PIPELINE-004=, and this clarification as one protocol set.

* REVIEW Canonical Identity Revisions

Government units and components are revisioned canonical subjects, not mutable references.

#+begin_src lisp
(defstruct government-unit-revision
  source-contract-id source-contract-version
  unit-id unit-revision-id prior-unit-revision-id
  supersedes-unit-revision-ids
  canonical-name unit-kind parent-unit-revision-id
  state-code state-fips county-geoid census-government-id
  active-status valid-from valid-through
  identity-evidence-ids created-at created-by)

(defstruct government-component-revision
  source-contract-id source-contract-version
  component-id component-revision-id prior-component-revision-id
  supersedes-component-revision-ids
  unit-revision-id canonical-name component-kind function
  authority-kind service-geography parent-component-revision-id
  identity-evidence-ids confidence valid-from valid-through
  created-at created-by)
#+end_src

Commands, jobs, profiles, search plans, decisions, assessments, reviews, and gates bind exact =unit-revision-id= and =component-revision-id= values. Logical IDs are used only to serialize creation and revision writes.

The Independent Review Authority accepts =unit= and =component= as subject kinds. A subject owner cannot review its own identity revision.

* REVIEW Applicability Contract Revisions

An applicability contract has both a stable logical ID and an immutable revision ID.

#+begin_src lisp
(defstruct route-applicability-contract-revision
  source-contract-id source-contract-version
  applicability-contract-id applicability-contract-revision-id
  prior-applicability-contract-revision-id
  supersedes-applicability-contract-revision-ids
  semantic-version jurisdiction-scope
  route-definition-revision-ids rule-revision-ids
  content-hash valid-from valid-through created-at created-by)
#+end_src

Every command, job, result, subject decision, assessment, review, and gate binds =applicability-contract-revision-id=. A semantic version string is descriptive and cannot substitute for the canonical revision ID.

The Independent Review Authority reviews that exact revision. Coverage gates pin both the applicability-contract revision and its current review revision.

* REVIEW Durable Evidence Delivery Intent

Artifact durability is not downstream delivery. Every retained artifact commit creates or confirms one durable evidence-delivery intent.

#+begin_src lisp
(defstruct government-data-evidence-delivery-intent
  intent-id artifact-id artifact-hash
  job-revision-id attempt-id fencing-token
  required-delivery-step-keys
  acknowledged-delivery-step-keys
  pending-delivery-step-keys
  state prior-intent-revision-id
  created-at updated-at)
#+end_src

Required delivery steps are explicit and idempotent. The baseline retained-artifact steps are:

1. =retrieval-event:<retrieval-event-id>=
2. =normalization-release:<normalization-work-id>=

Additional declared steps may include metadata extraction, provenance indexing, or quarantine. Retrieved content cannot add delivery steps or capabilities.

* REVIEW Commit and Recovery Sequence

The normative sequence is:

1. validate protocol, contracts, policy, immutable job, attempt, lease generation, and fence;
2. issue the bounded request;
3. stage, stream, bound, and hash artifact bytes;
4. atomically commit artifact bytes/metadata and the durable delivery intent when one transaction boundary exists;
5. otherwise commit through a durable outbox and run startup plus periodic orphan-artifact recovery;
6. deliver the exact retrieval-event write from the intent;
7. release the exact normalization work from the intent;
8. acknowledge each step idempotently;
9. emit evidence and proposals;
10. commit a checkpoint that records completed and pending delivery steps;
11. close the lease;
12. emit terminal success only after the intent is durable and every success-required step is acknowledged.

A crash after artifact commit never relies on an implicit "resume from artifact" convention. Recovery must recreate, reclaim, replay, complete, or explicitly quarantine the delivery intent.

* REVIEW Result and Checkpoint Fields

All government-data and adapter results add:

#+begin_src lisp
  evidence-delivery-intent-ids
  acknowledged-delivery-step-keys
  pending-delivery-step-keys
#+end_src

All retained-artifact checkpoints add:

#+begin_src lisp
  evidence-delivery-intent-ids
  acknowledged-delivery-step-keys
  pending-delivery-step-keys
  delivery-state-hash
#+end_src

A terminal =success= result is invalid when:

- a retained artifact has no durable intent;
- a success-required delivery step is pending;
- an acknowledgement references the wrong job, attempt, artifact, or fence;
- orphan-artifact recovery has unresolved work;
- a checkpoint claims work beyond acknowledged or explicitly pending delivery state.

* REVIEW Gate Identity Bindings

A jurisdiction gate additionally pins:

- exact unit and component revision IDs used by every route;
- current independent review revisions for those identity subjects;
- the exact applicability-contract revision ID and review revision;
- every evidence-delivery intent required by accepted artifacts;
- proof that no accepted artifact is orphaned, unaccounted, or pending a success-required step.

Identity, applicability, or delivery invalidation makes the gate non-current without mutating historical gate revisions.

* REVIEW Operational Metadata Route

=operational-metadata= remains route order 16 for deterministic gate ordering, but it is a derived cross-cutting gate assertion.

It does not independently emit acquisition jobs, source profiles, or network probes. It derives its assessment from the exact deployment, profile, access, lease, cursor, refresh, scope, provenance, truncation, artifact, delivery-intent, checkpoint, gap, and review revisions bound by routes 1 through 15.

The machine-readable applicability fixture must declare:

#+begin_example
"derived": true,
"emits_jobs": false,
"assessment_kind": "derived-gate-assertion"
#+end_example

A passed route-16 assertion is impossible while any required cross-cutting field is absent or stale.

* REVIEW Required Fixtures

The protocol set requires deterministic fixtures for:

- unit and component create, CAS conflict, supersession, and review invalidation;
- applicability-contract revision migration and review invalidation;
- artifact commit plus intent commit;
- crash after artifact commit before retrieval-event delivery;
- crash after retrieval-event delivery before normalization release;
- duplicate delivery acknowledgement;
- stale-fence delivery rejection;
- orphan-artifact recovery and quarantine;
- checkpoint pending-step accounting;
- terminal-success rejection with incomplete delivery;
- canonical gate hash equivalence and mismatch;
- gate expiry without an expiry write;
- identity, applicability, review, and delivery invalidation after an old gate;
- operational-metadata derived assertion without standalone jobs.

* REVIEW Compatibility

Protocol-v1 and earlier v1.1 records lacking explicit unit, component, applicability revision, or evidence-delivery-intent bindings are migration/read-only inputs. They cannot create current reviewed completion.

Migration creates new canonical subject revisions, review revisions, jobs, attempts, delivery intents where retained artifacts exist, assessments, and gates. Migration never fabricates an acknowledgement for work that was not durably delivered.

* REVIEW Acceptance

This clarification is satisfied only when:

1. one identity revision model is used across every design;
2. applicability semantic versions never substitute for canonical revision IDs;
3. result and checkpoint contracts expose delivery intent state;
4. adapter and parent protocol commit order are identical;
5. terminal success cannot hide pending delivery;
6. route 16 is machine-readable and derived;
7. deterministic fixtures and offline CI validate these rules.
