* Acquisition Commands

First inspect which subtitle tracks YouTube exposes:

#+begin_src sh
yt-dlp --list-subs 'https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SnbkIm56TfI'
#+end_src

Download manual captions when present and automatic captions otherwise, without downloading the video, and normalize them to SRT:

#+begin_src sh
yt-dlp \
  --skip-download \
  --write-subs \
  --write-auto-subs \
  --sub-langs 'en.*,en' \
  --sub-format 'vtt/best' \

* Research Questions

1. What exact geolocation or timestamping method does the video claim or demonstrate?
2. Which observations are extracted from the audio: thunder onset times, repeated thunder events, rain intensity, wind, sirens, aircraft, speech, or other environmental cues?
3. Can thunder events be matched to independently recorded lightning strikes or storm-cell observations with enough temporal resolution to reduce the candidate area?
4. What timing uncertainty is introduced by unknown upload delay, editing, cuts, variable playback speed, microphone latency, encoding, and absent recording timestamps?
5. How does the speed-of-sound delay between lightning and thunder affect the inference model?
6. What public weather, radar, lightning, and storm-report archives provide sufficient historical resolution for independent verification?
7. How many candidate storms or geographic regions remain after each constraint is applied?
8. What false-positive rate appears when the same method is tested against negative-control recordings from other storms?
9. What confidence language is justified: exact location, bounded region, time window, compatible-with, or unresolved?
10. Which evidence must be preserved so another analyst can replay the conclusion without trusting the actor's prose?

* Initial Hypothesis

Thunder audio can potentially act as a temporal and environmental signal, but a single thunder event is unlikely to identify a unique location by itself. The useful research question is whether multiple timed storm observations, recording metadata, visible context, and independent weather or lightning records combine to reduce the candidate set reproducibly.

This is a hypothesis for testing, not a finding.

* Evidence Model

Each research run should preserve:

- the target document and stable target ID
- source URL and retrieval timestamp
- yt-dlp metadata JSON
- exact subtitle or transcript artifact and subtitle track identity
- hashes of downloaded attachments
- audio extraction parameters
- timestamps for each candidate acoustic event
- any preprocessing or filtering parameters
- public weather or lightning source identifiers and retrieval timestamps
- candidate locations or regions before and after each constraint
- rejected candidates and rejection reasons
- confidence and unresolved ambiguity
- actor version, model version when applicable, and complete provenance chain

Derived spectrograms or clipped audio remain attachments derived from the target source and should point back to the original target and source artifact.

* Evaluation Criteria

A successful research pass must do more than produce a plausible map point.

It should:

1. reproduce the video's method from captured evidence rather than description alone
2. identify all external datasets required by the method
3. run at least one positive case when enough source data exists
4. run negative controls or ambiguity tests
5. quantify timing and spatial uncertainty
6. keep target, attachments, derived artifacts, and conclusions separately addressable
7. emit no precise-location claim when multiple candidates remain compatible with the evidence

* Scope Boundary

This node researches forensic analysis of publicly available media and general geolocation methodology. It does not authorize covert tracking of a private person, access-controlled data acquisition, or conversion of an ambiguous environmental signal into a precise personal-location claim without independent evidence.

* Footnotes and Glossary

- *Target* — A StarIntel document whose explicit creation or execution selects work for an actor.
- *Attachment* — Original or derived evidence supplied to a target-driven run without becoming an independent run trigger by itself.
- *Thunder delay* — The propagation delay between a lightning discharge and the later arrival of its acoustic thunder at the recorder.
- *Negative control* — A comparison case expected not to match the hypothesized source, used to measure false positives and ambiguity.
- *Candidate set* — The remaining locations, time windows, or storm events compatible with the evidence after applying current constraints.
