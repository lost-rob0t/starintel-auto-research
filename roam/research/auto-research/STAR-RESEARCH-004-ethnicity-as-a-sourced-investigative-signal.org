* Research Question

How should StarIntel track ethnicity[fn:ethnicity] as an investigative signal[fn:signal] while preserving the exact source claim, distinguishing self-description from third-party description or model inference, and preventing a weak observation from becoming false identity proof?

* Current Decision

Ethnicity is a signal worth tracking.

The field name should be =ethnicity=, not =race=. The immediate Nim compatibility fix renames the legacy =Person.race= field and its regression test to =Person.ethnicity=. This does not by itself amend the canonical StarIntel 0.9 document schema[fn:schema].

A final cross-language contract remains research work and must follow the repository document-contract process before becoming authoritative.

* Working Finding

A bare canonical string is insufficient for durable intelligence work. Ethnicity can be:

- self-described by the subject
- stated by an official or institutional source
- reported by a witness, journalist, biographer, or other third party
- copied from an earlier dataset
- translated from another classification system
- inferred by a human analyst
- inferred by a model from a name, language, location, image, family link, or other indirect evidence

Those origins are not equivalent. StarIntel should preserve the observation and its provenance[fn:provenance], not silently collapse every value into one permanent fact.

* Proposed Signal Contract to Research

Each ethnicity observation should be able to retain:

- the exact source label
- an optional normalized label
- whether it was self-described, source-stated, translated, analyst-inferred, or model-inferred
- the source document and evidence location
- observation and retrieval times
- the subject or entity the observation concerns
- confidence and the method used to derive it
- the vocabulary or classification system, when one exists
- conflicts with other observations
- review state
- validity or temporal scope when the claim is time-bound

The system should support multiple simultaneous observations. It should not force one value when sources conflict or when a person uses more than one description.

* Investigative Uses to Evaluate

Ethnicity may improve:

- search expansion across alternate spellings, transliterations, languages, and community-specific sources
- demographic analysis of organizations, networks, appointments, donations, representation, and outcomes
- entity-resolution candidate generation when combined with stronger independent signals
- detecting conflicting biographies or records
- selecting language, regional, archival, or community-source adapters
- identifying data-collection gaps and sampling bias

Ethnicity must not be treated as:

- a unique identifier
- sufficient evidence that two records describe the same person
- proof of nationality, citizenship, religion, language, political belief, criminality, or group membership
- a reason to discard contradictory evidence
- an unsourced label that overwrites the original source wording

* Required Research Tasks

1. Inspect the canonical StarIntel 0.9 Person and observation contracts.
2. Inventory how maintained Python, Common Lisp, Nim, and JavaScript implementations currently represent ethnicity or adjacent identity fields.
3. Compare public demographic vocabularies and determine whether StarIntel should normalize to one vocabulary, retain source vocabularies, or support mappings between them.
4. Define requiredness, null behavior, cardinality, mutability, conflict handling, merge behavior, and temporal semantics.
5. Decide whether ethnicity belongs directly on Person data, in a reusable sourced-observation document, or in both through derived projections.
6. Define how inferred values are marked and prevented from masquerading as source-stated facts.
7. Add shared positive, negative, conflict, round-trip, and migration fixtures.
8. Specify the migration from legacy =race= fields without silently relabeling ambiguous historical values.
9. Update every maintained language implementation only after the canonical contract is approved.

* Acceptance Criteria

This research can move to =REVIEW= when it provides:

- a concrete schema proposal
- an explicit provenance and conflict model
- a migration rule for legacy =race=
- cross-language implementation impact
- conformance fixtures
- query and indexing behavior
- documented limits on identity-resolution use
- examples covering self-description, sourced claims, conflicting claims, and inference

* Footnotes and Glossary

[fn:ethnicity] Ethnicity: A person's self-described or externally described cultural, ancestral, linguistic, regional, or historical group association. The term does not have one universal classification system.

[fn:signal] Investigative signal: A piece of information that can help generate, rank, test, or reject a research hypothesis but is not sufficient proof by itself.

[fn:provenance] Provenance: The recorded origin and handling history of information, including where it came from, when it was obtained, and how it was transformed.

[fn:schema] Document schema: A machine-readable contract defining which fields a StarIntel document may contain, the type of each field, and the validation rules applied to it.
