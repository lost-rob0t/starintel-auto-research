* Research Question

How can StarIntel obtain, preserve, normalize, and continuously refresh public data from every U.S. local government and the federal government without assuming a universal API, creating one brittle scraper per jurisdiction, or making unsupported claims of total completeness?

This pass defines the acquisition landscape and coverage model. A following pass must define the actors, manifests, messages, supervision trees, schedules, retries, and normalization flows that execute it.

* Executive Finding

There is no single endpoint for every local government and no stable list of every public data system operated by those governments.

The correct architecture is a coverage program with five layers:

1. build a canonical registry of government units
2. resolve official domains, aliases, boundaries, and external identifiers
3. discover and fingerprint each unit's public systems
4. route each discovered source to a reusable platform or format adapter
5. measure coverage, freshness, provenance, failures, and known gaps continuously

The 2025 Census Government Organization series reports 91,438 local governments. Creating one actor per government would produce a huge, duplicated, operationally incoherent system. Actors should instead be divided by responsibility and source family while bounded jobs carry the government-unit and source identity.

For federal coverage, StarIntel must combine an organization hierarchy with a domain registry, agency data inventories, cross-government systems, agency-specific APIs, bulk repositories, and ordinary official web sources. No one federal directory contains every bureau, office, commission, board, laboratory, field office, data system, and publication.

"Every government" and "every dataset" must be represented as measurable goals, not asserted as completed facts.

* Scope

** Included

- U.S. federal legislative, executive, and judicial public data
- independent federal establishments, commissions, boards, corporations, and sub-tier agencies
- state governments as a bridge between federal and local acquisition
- local county, municipal, township, special district, and school district governments
- dependent school systems and public pension systems where present in Census organization files
- territorial, tribal, and interstate public sources discoverable through official registries
- official APIs, bulk files, feeds, catalogs, structured web pages, PDFs, and public vendor systems
- current and historical public records
- source discovery, acquisition, preservation, normalization, provenance, and coverage auditing
- public-records request tracking for known but unpublished data

** Not Equivalent to This Scope

- scraping every page on every government website
- bypassing authentication, access controls, CAPTCHAs, or paid portals
- collecting protected personal information
- treating a vendor portal as authoritative without linking it to a government unit
- treating Data.gov as the complete raw-data store
- treating a .gov domain list as the complete government-unit registry
- treating a map boundary as proof of organizational identity
- treating a successful HTTP response as complete acquisition
- using OCR when a structured or text-bearing source exists

* Definition of Exhaustive Coverage

Coverage has several independent dimensions.

| Dimension | Meaning |
|-----------+---------|
| Unit coverage | Every known government unit has a canonical registry record |
| Identity coverage | Aliases, identifiers, parent relationships, status, and validity dates are resolved |
| Domain coverage | Official web domains and public vendor-hosted systems are linked to the unit |
| Source-profile coverage | Each unit has a tested inventory of catalogs, APIs, feeds, portals, documents, and unsupported systems |
| Data-family coverage | Required families such as meetings, finance, procurement, GIS, and codes are assessed separately |
| Temporal coverage | Current data and retained history are measured independently |
| Freshness coverage | Sources have schedules and last-success timestamps appropriate to their update rate |
| Provenance coverage | Every normalized record resolves to a captured source and retrieval event |
| Failure coverage | Blocked, missing, ambiguous, retired, and records-request-only sources are explicit |
| Validation coverage | Counts, hashes, pages, cursors, and reconciliation runs detect silent loss |

A unit can therefore be fully registered but have poor source coverage. A source can be discovered but inaccessible. A dataset can be acquired but stale. A normalized record can exist while its provenance is broken. These states must never be collapsed into a single boolean.

* Canonical Local-Government Universe

The U.S. Census Bureau Government Units Listing is the primary national identity spine for state and local governments.

The 2025 release includes a comprehensive organization public-use file for active independent government units and related public organizations. The national count increased from 90,837 local governments in 2022 to 91,438 in 2025.

The five Census local-government types are:

- county
- municipal
- township
- special district
- independent school district

The registry must preserve Census identifiers, names, government type, state, county or area references, functional classifications, active status, and release vintage.

The annual organization files provide a repeatable baseline, but they do not provide all official domains, all agencies below each unit, every dependent body, or all public systems. Continuous source discovery is still required.

** Adjacent Local Public Organizations

The acquisition registry should also represent, without confusing them with independent local governments:

- dependent school systems
- public pension systems
- housing authorities
- transit authorities
- water, sewer, fire, library, hospital, port, airport, and development authorities
- councils of governments and regional planning organizations
- election boards
- courts and clerks
- public universities and community-college districts
- joint powers and interstate bodies
- tribal governments and subordinate entities
- territorial governments and instrumentalities

Each record must declare whether it is an independent government, dependent organization, component, authority, service district, or unresolved public body.

* Federal Organization Universe

The federal identity spine should combine several official sources.

** SAM.gov Federal Hierarchy

The public Federal Hierarchy supplies departments or independent agencies and their public sub-tier relationships. It is authoritative for federal funding and awarding organizations.

The public hierarchy is useful for department and sub-tier identity, but it cannot be treated as a complete public office directory. More detailed federal-only hierarchy data has separate access restrictions and cannot be used to publish a public reconstruction.

** CISA .gov Domain Data

CISA publishes a daily list of all registered .gov domains and a daily DNS zone file. Records include domain type, organization, location, and a security contact.

This is a high-value discovery and change-detection source for federal, interstate, state, territorial, tribal, county, city, special district, and school district domains.

It is not a complete government registry because:

- some governments use .us, educational, or commercial domains
- one government may own many domains
- one domain may serve many components
- vendor portals often use vendor domains
- registration names can differ from canonical organization names
- domain possession does not prove that every hosted source is current or authoritative

** Agency and Component Directories

Additional federal enumeration should use:

- USA.gov agency directory
- FOIA.gov agency and component records
- agency organizational charts and directories
- Congress.gov committees, members, legislation, hearings, and votes
- GovInfo collections and United States Government Manual
- Federal Register agency references
- agency-specific data inventories and developer pages

The resulting registry must support departments, sub-tiers, offices, components, commissions, boards, corporations, laboratories, field offices, inspector generals, courts, legislative bodies, and retired organizations with validity intervals.

* Identity and Crosswalk Model

No single identifier spans all levels and source families. StarIntel needs an identity graph.

A government or component record should support:

- StarIntel stable object identifier
- Census Government Units Listing identifier and vintage
- Census GEOID or FIPS codes where applicable
- state-local government type
- NCES local education agency identifier
- SAM Federal Hierarchy department and sub-tier codes
- federal agency and bureau codes
- USAspending agency identifiers
- Unique Entity Identifier when publicly associated with an entity
- official .gov and non-.gov domains
- FOIA.gov component identifier
- vendor tenant, client, portal, or dataset identifiers
- boundary artifact identifiers
- canonical name
- alternate names, abbreviations, predecessor and successor names
- parent, dependent-on, serves, overlaps, and administered-by relationships
- valid-from and valid-through dates
- active, inactive, merged, dissolved, renamed, or unresolved status
- source confidence and reconciliation state

Identity resolution must retain contradictory candidates instead of silently choosing one.

* Bootstrap and Enrichment Sources

** Census Government Organization

Use the newest annual Government Units Listing as the canonical local-unit baseline. Retain prior vintages to detect creation, dissolution, reclassification, and naming changes.

** TIGER/Line and TIGERweb

Use legal and statistical geographies for spatial crosswalks involving states, counties, places, county subdivisions, school districts, tribal areas, and related geography.

Do not assume every special district or administrative organization has an equivalent TIGER boundary.

** NCES Common Core of Data

Use the annual education-agency universe for local education agency identifiers, operational status, schools, and education-specific crosswalks.

** Census Building Permits Survey Universe

Use permit-issuing jurisdictions as an enrichment source for identifying building-permit systems and the governments or areas they serve.

** Government Finance and Employment Collections

Use Census state and local finance, employment, and payroll files as national validation and enrichment sources.

These national collections may be sampled outside Census of Governments years and must not replace direct jurisdiction-level records when complete unit data is required.

* Source Discovery Pipeline

For every canonical government unit or federal component, source discovery should proceed in bounded stages.

1. import known official domains from authoritative registries
2. resolve homepages and redirects
3. inspect DNS, TLS certificates, HTTP headers, redirects, HTML metadata, and linked assets
4. discover sitemaps, robots files, RSS or Atom feeds, calendars, data inventories, OpenAPI documents, and developer pages
5. search official-domain pages for data, open data, GIS, meetings, agendas, minutes, legislation, code, budget, finance, procurement, permits, records, and FOIA
6. identify external vendor links and tenant or client identifiers
7. fingerprint platform-specific paths, JavaScript bundles, response headers, API shapes, and embedded configuration
8. test candidate endpoints with read-only requests
9. record source capabilities, pagination, update markers, authentication, rate limits, formats, and legal or policy notes
10. create explicit gaps for expected data families that were not found
11. revisit unresolved and changed profiles on a schedule

Discovery should record how each candidate was found. Search-engine results alone are not sufficient provenance; the final profile must resolve to an official domain, official document, or clearly delegated public vendor system.

* Acquisition Priority

Use the least fragile and most complete available method in this order:

1. official bulk export or static archive
2. documented official API
3. official machine-readable inventory, catalog, or feed
4. public vendor API delegated by the government
5. structured HTML, embedded JSON, JSON-LD, or downloadable table
6. text-bearing PDF, spreadsheet, database, GIS service, or document archive
7. browser execution for JavaScript-only public systems
8. OCR only when no text-bearing representation exists
9. records request or manual escalation for known but unpublished data

Lower-priority methods should not be used merely because they are easier to prototype. For example, a web-page scraper should not replace an available bulk file or stable API.

* Catalog and Metadata Discovery

** Data.gov Catalog API

The current Data.gov Catalog API v4 exposes metadata from federal, state, local, and tribal publishers. It replaces the legacy CKAN endpoint for new development and requires an api.data.gov key for production automation.

Data.gov is a discovery and metadata aggregation layer. Dataset distributions usually point to agency or jurisdiction resources that must be acquired separately.

** DCAT-US and data.json

Federal agencies publish enterprise data inventories using DCAT-US, commonly at =/data.json=. Many state and local governments use the same pattern.

Acquisition must support:

- DCAT-US v1.1
- DCAT-US v3 records and data-service descriptions
- malformed but recoverable catalogs
- catalogs split across bureaus or domains
- distributions representing files, APIs, services, and landing pages
- harvest timestamps and source hashes
- tombstones when distributions disappear

DCAT-US 3.0 exists, but its implementation guidance was still marked draft in May 2026. Supporting both major schemas is safer than assuming immediate uniform migration.

** Generic Machine-Readable Probes

For each official domain, bounded discovery should test or locate:

- =/data.json=
- =/data=
- =/open-data=
- =/datasets=
- =/api=
- =/swagger.json=
- =/openapi.json=
- =/api-docs=
- =/sitemap.xml=
- RSS and Atom links
- iCalendar feeds
- JSON-LD
- Socrata, ArcGIS, CKAN, Huwise or OpenDataSoft, and custom catalog signatures

Blind path spraying must be capped and deduplicated per host.

Adapters should be designed around source families, not individual cities or counties.

The National Archives explicitly warns against attempting to download the entire Catalog through its API, so full-corpus work must use an approved bulk path instead.

** Legal-Publication Provenance

A convenient API representation is not always the legal publication of record.

For regulatory and statutory material, normalized records should retain:

- source system
- rendition type
- official or informational status
- publication date
- effective date
- amendment history
- package, granule, document, docket, or citation identifiers
- link to an official edition where available

For example, FederalRegister.gov is useful for structured access, while GovInfo supplies official Federal Register editions and related bulk collections. The graph should preserve both and distinguish their roles.

* Acquisition Contract

Every acquisition job should declare:

- canonical government or component identifier
- source-profile identifier and version
- source URL or service identifier
- expected platform and data family
- allowed HTTP methods and capabilities
- credential reference when legitimately required
- rate and concurrency limits
- pagination or bulk-transfer strategy
- incremental cursor, timestamp, ETag, or version
- maximum requests, bytes, objects, and wall-clock time
- accepted media types
- raw-artifact retention policy
- normalization schema
- cancellation identifier
- configuration hash

A source may not expand its own scope or grant itself new credentials.

* Raw Capture and Provenance

Every retrieval should produce an immutable acquisition event and, when permitted, a retained raw artifact.

Minimum provenance:

- retrieval identifier
- government-unit or federal-component identifier
- source-profile identifier
- requested URL and final URL
- request method and bounded request metadata
- response timestamp and status
- content type and content length
- ETag, Last-Modified, checksum, and source version when available
- selected response headers
- TLS and redirect observations when material
- raw artifact URI
- SHA-256 content hash
- actor, adapter, and configuration version
- page, cursor, object, or range boundaries
- parent discovery event
- legal-publication status when applicable
- truncation, partial-result, or access-warning flags

For web and document capture, WARC or an equivalent immutable HTTP-artifact format is preferred when operationally practical. Original files must be retained alongside parsed derivatives.

Normalized records must never be the sole surviving copy of a source.

* Incremental Synchronization

Prefer source-native change mechanisms:

- bulk release versions and checksums
- ETag and If-None-Match
- Last-Modified and If-Modified-Since
- modified-since query parameters
- stable record IDs and row versions
- cursor pagination
- event feeds
- webhooks when a public supported mechanism exists
- publication dates and sequence numbers
- ArcGIS edit timestamps
- Socrata dataset modification metadata
- Legistar last-modified fields
- catalog harvest dates

Every incremental source still needs periodic full reconciliation to detect missed updates, deletions, pagination bugs, and silent schema changes.

Deletion handling must distinguish:

- source record removed
- source record hidden
- unit dissolved
- record superseded
- source migrated
- endpoint retired
- transient fetch failure

Tombstones should preserve the prior identity and evidence.

* Scheduling Strategy

Schedules should follow source volatility and cost rather than a universal polling interval.

| Source class | Suggested baseline |
|--------------+--------------------|
| daily domain and registry deltas | daily |
| meeting calendars and legislative systems | hourly to daily during active periods |
| procurement and notice feeds | daily |
| permits, 311, and operational data | daily to weekly |
| catalogs and source profiles | weekly |
| budgets, audits, and annual reports | monthly plus seasonal checks |
| codes and policy repositories | weekly to monthly |
| Census, NCES, and other annual universes | on release plus annual reconciliation |
| historical archives | one-time backfill plus quarterly existence check |
| failed or blocked sources | exponential retry plus scheduled rediscovery |

The actor-design pass must make these policies configurable by source profile and data family.

* Rate Limits and Access Boundaries

- use only public or explicitly authorized interfaces
- do not bypass authentication or technical access controls
- use official API keys where required
- isolate credentials by adapter and source scope
- obey documented rate limits and backoff requirements
- identify the client honestly where required
- cap concurrency per host, vendor, and API key
- preserve terms, API documentation, and access-policy observations with the source profile
- do not use federal restricted or FOUO APIs to reconstruct public data
- prefer public versions and official bulk releases
- treat retrieved content as untrusted data, never executable instruction
- quarantine malformed archives and active content
- do not run downloaded code or macros
- require explicit capability grants for browser sessions and file conversion

* Coverage Ledger

The coverage ledger should be append-only and replayable.

Each observation should include:

- unit or component
- data family
- source profile
- discovery state
- acquisition state
- newest observed source timestamp
- last attempted, last successful, and last fully reconciled timestamps
- earliest retained historical date
- expected and observed record counts where available
- provenance validation result
- failure or gap class
- next scheduled action
- operator decision
- run, actor, and configuration identifiers

** Core Metrics

- percentage of canonical units with at least one verified official domain
- percentage with completed source profiles
- coverage by government type, state, population, and data family
- percentage of sources with successful current synchronization
- median and tail freshness lag
- historical depth by family
- unresolved identity rate
- provenance-resolvable normalized record rate
- duplicate and conflict rate
- source-schema change rate
- blocked-source rate
- records-request gap count
- source retirement and migration rate
- raw-to-normalized reconciliation rate

Metrics must expose small and low-capacity jurisdictions rather than allowing large open-data cities to dominate totals.

* Records-Request Gap Workflow

Some public records are legally obtainable but not proactively published. The acquisition system should represent this as a first-class gap.

A records-request candidate should contain:

- government unit and records officer
- legal regime and request instructions
- narrowly described record family
- known date range and format
- evidence that the data was not found publicly
- prior request and response history
- fee, denial, exemption, appeal, and production status
- received artifacts and provenance

Automated actors may prepare and track requests, but sending requests or accepting fees should remain an explicit operator action unless separately authorized.

* Validation and Reconciliation

Validation should occur at several levels.

** Transport

- status and media type match expectations
- page and cursor sequences terminate
- byte and object counts stay within declared limits
- hashes are stable for identical artifacts

** Source

- dataset or service identity matches the source profile
- schema and fields are recorded
- timestamps and version semantics are understood
- official or delegated authority is documented

** Record

- required identifiers and dates are present
- source locators resolve
- duplicates are detected without deleting conflicting evidence
- parse warnings are retained

** Cross-Source

- registry counts reconcile with release documentation
- domain ownership and organization identity are checked
- national aggregate files are compared with direct local data
- predecessor and successor relationships are consistent
- records from informational APIs link to official editions when required

** Replay

Given the same raw artifacts, parser version, and configuration, normalization should reproduce the same output hashes.

* Negative Findings

- No universal local-government API exists.
- The Census government list identifies governments but not all their data systems.
- The .gov registry identifies domains but not all governments or delegated vendor portals.
- Data.gov is not a complete raw-data mirror.
- Federal Hierarchy public data is not a complete public office directory.
- GIS geography cannot replace organizational identity.
- National finance and employment collections cannot replace direct records for every unit and year.
- A vendor brand does not guarantee one stable API across all tenants.
- A successful scrape does not prove historical completeness.
- Actor-per-jurisdiction is the wrong scaling boundary.
- Browser automation should not be the default acquisition method.
- OCR should not be the default document method.
- Completeness cannot be reduced to one percentage without hiding important gaps.

* Recommendation

Approve this research as the acquisition basis for a separate actor-design pass.

The design should preserve ADARD ownership of research runs and evidence while allowing supervised acquisition actors to execute bounded jobs through versioned envelopes. Government identity, source profiles, coverage state, raw artifacts, and normalized records must remain durable StarIntel data rather than private state inside individual crawlers.

The first measurable milestone should not be "all data downloaded." It should be:

- all 2025 Census local-government units registered
- federal department and sub-tier baseline registered
- CISA .gov registry ingested
- source-profile schema implemented
- a statistically and structurally diverse pilot across every local-government type
- federal cross-government catalog and publication sources profiled
- platform frequencies measured
- coverage gaps reported without being hidden

* Footnotes and Glossary

[fn:government-unit] Government unit: A legally organized public entity represented in an authoritative registry, distinct from a website, department, dataset, or vendor tenant.

[fn:source-profile] Source profile: A versioned description of one public data source, including ownership, platform, capabilities, formats, pagination, update semantics, limits, and acquisition history.

[fn:coverage-ledger] Coverage ledger: Append-only observations describing what is known, acquired, current, missing, blocked, or unresolved for each unit and data family.

[fn:identity-spine] Identity spine: The canonical set of organization records and crosswalks used to attach domains, sources, datasets, people, boundaries, and historical changes to the same government entity.

[fn:delegated-source] Delegated source: A public system hosted by a vendor or partner that a government uses as an official publication or transaction surface.

[fn:tombstone] Tombstone: A durable record that a source object or organization was removed, retired, merged, or superseded without erasing its prior identity or provenance.
