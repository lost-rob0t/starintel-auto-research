* User Request

The user requested a child-abuse research architecture using actors, not an AutoDig run.

The user's explicit requirements are:

1. do not expose child names;
2. do not search for child names;
3. do not create named child records;
4. do not connect a child's identity across cases or datasets;
5. do not run AutoDig on child-abuse cases;
6. do not automatically crawl, enumerate, ingest, or publish real cases in this phase;
7. design actors and policies only;
8. permit adult identity work only for publicly documented adult defendants or offenders and only under strict legal-status and evidence gates;
9. treat child-name exposure as additional harm to the child;
10. fail closed whenever age, role, privacy, or legal status is uncertain.

This request is normative and overrides any generic StarIntel preference for preserving public names. A child's name remains prohibited even when another public source has already published it.

* Research Boundary

This project is not a national child-name dataset and is not an NCANDS-to-name resolver.

The National Child Abuse and Neglect Data System, or NCANDS[fn:ncands], collects annual state submissions about child-abuse and neglect reports. The public federal products are statistical reports, while restricted-use files are intended for approved research. NCANDS may inform categories, aggregate trends, and evaluation strata. It must not be treated as a public identity source.

The National Incident-Based Reporting System, or NIBRS[fn:nibrs], provides incident-level crime context such as offense, victim, known-offender, relationship, and arrest information. It is useful for aggregate and method research, but this actor-design phase does not download or process NIBRS records.

The National Vital Statistics System, or NVSS[fn:nvss], provides mortality statistics based on death certificates. It may support aggregate fatality validation, but public mortality files are not a child-identity source for this project.

State and local public sources may later document severe cases through:

- prosecutor or district-attorney releases;
- criminal complaints, indictments, judgments, and sentencing records;
- police or sheriff releases;
- medical-examiner or coroner findings where public;
- state child-fatality or near-fatality disclosures;
- child-death-review reports;
- inspector-general or ombudsman reports;
- established local reporting that directly attributes facts to accessible official records.

This research node does not authorize retrieving those cases. It only defines the source classes an eventual operator-authorized actor could use.

* Case Scope

A future implementation may consider only severe cases involving at least one of:

- child fatality caused by abuse or neglect;
- near fatality;
- serious bodily injury;
- permanent physical impairment;
- severe starvation, poisoning, burning, torture, or medical neglect documented by authoritative evidence;
- charged sexual abuse where the adult defendant and legal case are public.

Ordinary family disputes, unsubstantiated referrals, custody allegations, school discipline, minor injuries, anonymous accusations, and social-media claims are outside scope.

A serious case is not automatically publishable. Severity is an eligibility condition for adult-case research, not evidence that a named adult committed the conduct.

* Identity Boundary

The only possible named target is a confirmed adult whose public legal role is supported by authoritative evidence.

Allowed adult statuses:

- charged;
- indicted;
- pleaded guilty or no contest;
- convicted;
- sentenced;
- acquitted or dismissed when preserving a complete case lifecycle;
- adult named in a final public agency finding when the source is legally public and the finding is represented exactly.

Disallowed named targets:

- child victims or survivors;
- minor alleged offenders;
- siblings;
- witnesses who are minors;
- nonoffending caregivers unless independently public and indispensable to the legal record;
- anonymous suspects;
- adults named only in allegations, family-court claims, rumors, social posts, or copied stories;
- adults whose age cannot be established as at least eighteen at the event.

* No-AutoDig Requirement

No actor in this design may autonomously start case discovery.

The following are prohibited until the user separately authorizes an implementation and a specific bounded run:

- national crawling;
- state-by-state crawling;
- query generation against live search engines;
- archive recovery;
- court scraping;
- prosecutor or police feed monitoring;
- NCANDS, NIBRS, or NVSS download and ingestion;
- automatic issue creation from cases;
- AutoDig dataset creation;
- named-person enrichment;
- public-record request submission;
- lifecycle monitoring of real defendants;
- publication to StarIntel, Quasar, GitHub Pages, or any public dataset.

Only static actor contracts, schemas, synthetic fixtures, privacy tests, and threat models are authorized by this request.

* Recommended Architecture Direction

The architecture should separate:

1. operator authorization;
2. severe-case eligibility;
3. adult-status verification;
4. child-identity removal;
5. source retrieval;
6. adult role and legal-status extraction;
7. evidence review;
8. publication policy.

The Child Identity Firewall must execute before and after every actor that handles source content. The adult identity resolver must never receive a child name as input.

* Evidence Standard

A future adult record must preserve the exact procedural status supported by evidence. Arrest, charge, indictment, plea, conviction, sentence, acquittal, and dismissal are separate events.

An adult name may be published only when:

- adult status is established;
- the legal role is explicit;
- the source is authoritative or independently corroborated;
- the source packet is redacted of child identifiers;
- a privacy reviewer approves the packet;
- a legal-status reviewer approves the packet;
- no material contradiction remains;
- the publication gate verifies that no child identifier appears in any field, label, excerpt, URL title, attachment title, or generated text.

* Threat Model

Primary risks:

- publishing a child's name already visible in a source;
- leaking a name through URLs, titles, image captions, PDF metadata, or logs;
- identifying a child through exact age, school, address, family structure, or medical narrative;
- connecting the same child across multiple cases;
- naming a minor alleged offender;
- naming an adult before formal public legal action;
- confusing a nonoffending caregiver, witness, or reporter with an offender;
- preserving graphic details that provide no analytical value;
- model-generated reconstruction of a redacted name;
- AutoDig expanding from one case into relatives, schools, associates, or social accounts.

The architecture must make these failures structurally impossible or cause the branch to stop.

* Recommendation

Proceed with actor and policy design only.

Do not create an AutoDig dataset. Do not select real cases. Do not run live retrieval. Do not produce names.

The first implementation, if separately approved later, should contain only:

- protocol schemas;
- deterministic synthetic fixtures;
- child-identifier detectors;
- redaction logic;
- export scanning;
- adult-age and legal-status validators;
- fake retrieval adapters;
- failure-closed supervisor tests.

A live source adapter must not be implemented or enabled until the privacy suite demonstrates zero child-identity leakage across every fixture.

* Footnotes and Glossary

[fn:ncands] National Child Abuse and Neglect Data System, or NCANDS: A voluntary federal collection of annual state information about reports of child abuse and neglect, used for national statistical reporting and approved research.

[fn:nibrs] National Incident-Based Reporting System, or NIBRS: The Federal Bureau of Investigation system that records structured details about individual crime incidents reported by participating law-enforcement agencies.

[fn:nvss] National Vital Statistics System, or NVSS: The national system that compiles official birth and death statistics from state vital-records systems.
