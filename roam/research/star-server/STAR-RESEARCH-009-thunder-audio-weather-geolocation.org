* Transcript-Derived Experiment

The transcript describes a concrete blind geolocation experiment.

** Inputs

1. a recording containing a sequence of thunder events;
2. lightning observations containing strike position and strike time;
3. a search region and time window, possibly as broad as the whole Earth for one known day;
4. no location supplied to the scoring pipeline during the blind test.

The presenter first had difficulty finding footage with both known location and reliable recording date, then switched to geolocatable live bird-feeder cameras because they consistently carried audio. A lightning event near one of those cameras triggered recording, producing paired audio and strike data for later blind evaluation.

** Lightning acquisition described by the transcript

The historical map exposed static image tiles, making OCR/reconstruction incomplete when strike markers overlapped. The presenter therefore inspected the live page, found a WebSocket, and observed per-strike messages containing latitude, longitude, time, and additional fields. A Python client emulated the browser connection and logged events continuously on a small AWS host.

The auto-captioned source name near the end of the video is rendered as “Blitzer tongue.” The behavior described by the transcript strongly matches Blitzortung.org / LightningMaps.org: Blitzortung's public forum documents a real-time WebSocket stream and fields including timing-deviation information, and describes the network as primarily oriented toward cloud-to-ground ground-point location. Treat the source identification as a high-confidence inference until video metadata or visible page text confirms the exact site.

** Modeled arrival time

For every candidate observer location and lightning event, the experiment predicts when thunder should arrive.

The video uses:

- a constant sound speed of =343 m/s=;
- propagation delay computed from source-to-observer distance divided by sound speed;
- slant range rather than horizontal range;
- an assumed acoustic source height of about =2 km= for cloud-to-ground strikes;
- no explicit wind or atmospheric correction in the proof of concept.

The source-height assumption is a research variable, not a validated constant. A lightning channel is spatially extended, and real thunder arrival structure depends on channel geometry, atmosphere, terrain, and propagation. Auto Research must test sensitivity to source height rather than inherit =2 km= as fact.

** Modeled amplitude

The proof of concept uses distance-based inverse-square decay for relative loudness and caps the close-range value to approximate microphone clipping. Because YouTube audio does not provide calibrated absolute sound pressure level, scoring uses relative amplitude rather than absolute decibels.

This is deliberately a crude forward model. Research must test whether timing carries most of the discriminative signal and whether the amplitude model improves ranking after microphone gain control, compression, attenuation, directionality, buildings, and terrain are considered.

** Observed thunder signal

The transcript says the observed audio is transformed into a one-dimensional thunder-energy curve by:

1. band-pass filtering approximately =20–250 Hz=;
2. measuring root-mean-square energy over time;
3. normalizing within the candidate clip/window relative to the loudest observed event;
4. smoothing the curve to tolerate modest timing-grid mismatch.

The frequency band is physically plausible but must not be treated as universal. Few et al. reported a broad maximum near 200 Hz with zero-crossing peaks around 180–260 Hz in one classic data set, while Holmes et al. found peak power from below 4 Hz to 125 Hz across forty intracloud and cloud-to-ground events, with mean peaks around 28 Hz for intracloud and 50 Hz for cloud-to-ground thunder. The research branch must therefore sweep filter bands instead of assuming 20–250 Hz is globally optimal.

** Unknown-time search

When exact recording time is withheld, every candidate location receives a time sweep. The transcript's proof of concept uses approximately five-second increments over the day. At every offset it compares the normalized observed curve with the normalized modeled curve for the same clip window.

This window-local normalization is important: the model must not normalize against lightning before or after the recording because the observed clip cannot contain those events.

** Similarity and acceleration

The presenter uses cosine similarity between the modeled and observed energy curves. Smoothing makes the score less brittle when the discrete time step or spatial grid misses exact alignment. The transcript then describes Fast Fourier Transform acceleration for evaluating similarity across many time offsets.

The initial global run described in the transcript uses:

- known date: July 2;
- unknown time;
- unknown location;
- roughly 30 minutes of audio;
- roughly fifteen audible events;
- a coarse =5 km= global grid;
- a prefilter requiring at least ten candidate strikes in a 30-minute window;
- a land-only filter for that test;
- about 90,000 surviving spatial points;
- about five-second time increments;
- roughly 700 million candidate checks by the presenter's approximation.

* Transcript-Derived Claimed Results

These are source claims to reproduce, not accepted StarIntel findings.

1. The real Central Florida region reportedly separated strongly from unrelated global candidates.
2. A coarse global pass reportedly recovered the correct city from about thirty minutes of thunder audio.
3. A second, finer spatial and temporal pass reportedly reduced the result to likely neighborhoods.
4. The presenter reports that the single best-scoring point was within =5 km= of the real camera location while deliberately withholding that location from viewers.
5. A longer-horizon test reportedly searched about three weeks when the general area was known but the exact day was not, with similar qualitative results.
6. The presenter says free data had roughly =1–5 km= event-location uncertainty and was mainly useful for cloud-to-ground events with good separation.
7. The presenter claims commercial data can improve event location toward roughly =100 m=, potentially allowing denser storms and shorter clips.

The final commercial-data claim needs geographic qualification. Vaisala states that the U.S. National Lightning Detection Network has median location accuracy better than 100 m in the continental United States. Vaisala's global GLD360 product instead reports about 1 km median location accuracy. “Paid data gives 100 m globally” is therefore not supported.

* Actual Auto Research Branches

The target should fan out into bounded evidence-producing branches instead of one generic “research thunder” prompt.

** Branch A — Source and lightning-feed contract

- identify the exact lightning service shown in the video;
- document WebSocket endpoint discovery, message fields, timestamp units, coordinate fields, event type, quality/deviation fields, connection requirements, retention and permitted use;
- distinguish Blitzortung live-stream behavior from LightningMaps presentation behavior;
- determine what historical replay is available without scraping rendered image tiles;
- preserve raw event messages and schema samples as attachments.

** Branch B — Acoustic forward model

Compare progressively richer models:

1. horizontal distance + constant sound speed;
2. slant distance with source-height sweep;
3. extended lightning-channel geometry;
4. temperature/humidity/wind-adjusted effective sound speed;
5. optional terrain/building attenuation and propagation effects.

Measure whether added physics improves blind location rank enough to justify complexity.

** Branch C — Thunder detector and signal representation

Evaluate:

- =20–250 Hz= RMS envelope baseline;
- alternate low-frequency bands suggested by the literature;
- multiband envelopes rather than one band;
- event/onset detection;
- robust wind rejection;
- microphone clipping and automatic-gain-control detection;
- spectrogram embeddings only as a later comparison, not as a replacement for the transparent baseline.

** Branch D — Search and scorer

Reproduce:

- spatial grid generation;
- candidate-strike density prefilter;
- unknown-time sweep;
- within-window normalization;
- smoothing;
- cosine similarity;
- FFT/cross-correlation acceleration;
- coarse-to-fine spatial refinement around top candidates.

The implementation must record the complete score distribution, not only the winning point.

** Branch E — Statistical validity and false positives

Measure:

- rank of ground truth;
- distance from top-1 candidate to ground truth;
- top-k radius and candidate count;
- margin between the correct region and best wrong region;
- score calibration under wrong-day and wrong-region controls;
- false-match rate produced by isolated large peaks;
- sensitivity to strike-location jitter, missing events, false detections, source-height error, filter band, time step, smoothing width, and clip duration.

** Branch F — Blind reproduction

Build a validation corpus from public cameras or operator-controlled recordings where ground truth is retained by the evaluator but hidden from the scoring branch.

Minimum fixtures:

- A: known-location, known-day sheltered microphone, location hidden from scorer;
- B: same observed clip scored against intentionally wrong regions;
- C: correct region with intentionally wrong days;
- D: wind or other low-frequency noise without thunder;
- E: dense storm dominated by intracloud activity, expected to stress free CG-oriented lightning data;
- F: clipped/compressed microphone audio;
- G: shortened clips at 20, 10, and 5 minutes where sufficient events exist.

A single successful replay is not enough. The result must report distributions across fixtures.

* Reproduction Gate

The video's headline result is promoted from source claim to StarIntel finding only if a blind reproduction satisfies all of the following:

1. scorer receives no ground-truth coordinates;
2. target date/time priors are recorded before execution;
3. lightning data are preserved with source and quality fields;
4. ground truth lands within the declared top-k confidence region;
5. for the claimed =5 km= result, the top candidate is within 5 km in at least one faithful reproduction and the result is not selected after tuning on that same fixture;
6. negative controls produce materially lower confidence or explicit ambiguity;
7. parameter choices are frozen before final evaluation or evaluated on a separate holdout set;
8. score margin and candidate-set size are reported, not only top-1 distance;
9. failures remain failures and are retained as evidence.

* Immediate Research Questions

1. Is the transcript's unnamed lightning service definitely Blitzortung/LightningMaps?
2. What exact WebSocket message schema and timestamp precision are exposed today?
3. How should =mds=, =mcg=, station count, and any other quality fields become probabilistic spatial uncertainty instead of a fixed 1–5 km error constant?
4. Does a 20–250 Hz RMS envelope outperform narrower, wider, and multiband representations on holdout recordings?
5. How much ranking accuracy comes from event timing versus relative amplitude?
6. Does a fixed =2 km= source height improve or degrade arrival-time prediction compared with an optimized or channel-aware model?
7. What clip length and number of distinct thunder events are needed for stable separation?
8. How often does a global time sweep generate a high accidental cosine match from unrelated storms?
9. How does free Blitzortung performance vary by station density and region?
10. What is the empirical gain from NLDN-grade or other higher-accuracy data when evaluated without changing the scoring target after seeing ground truth?
11. Can coarse-to-fine search reduce compute without changing top-k recall?
12. What confidence model converts raw similarity scores into an honest region rather than a falsely precise point?

* Footnotes and Glossary

- *Target* — A StarIntel document whose explicit creation or execution selects work for an actor.
- *Attachment* — Original or derived evidence supplied to a target-driven run without becoming an independent run trigger by itself.
- *Root-mean-square (RMS) envelope* — A time series summarizing signal energy over successive audio windows.
- *Slant range* — Three-dimensional source-to-observer distance rather than map-plane distance alone.
- *Cosine similarity* — Normalized vector similarity used here to compare temporal energy patterns without rewarding raw overall magnitude alone.
- *Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)* — An efficient discrete Fourier transform algorithm that can accelerate correlation-like calculations across many time offsets.
- *Negative control* — A comparison case expected not to match the target, used to expose accidental high scores and overfitting.
- *Holdout* — A fixture not used to choose parameters, reserved for evaluating whether the method generalizes.
- *Candidate set* — Locations and time offsets still compatible with the current evidence.
- *Ground truth* — Recorder location and time retained by the evaluator and hidden from the scoring branch until evaluation completes.
