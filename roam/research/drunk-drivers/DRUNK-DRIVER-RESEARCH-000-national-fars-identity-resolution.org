* Research Question

Can StarIntel use public web, news, court, prosecutor, police, open-data, and archival sources to resolve anonymous Fatality Analysis Reporting System records[fn:fars] to named impaired-driving defendants while keeping false identifications below an operationally acceptable level?

A successful system must do more than find a page containing a name. It must separately establish:

1. that a source describes the same crash;
2. that the named person occupied the relevant driver role;
3. that the source supports the claimed legal status;
4. that the identity remains sufficiently corroborated after contradictory evidence is considered.

* Executive Finding

The task is feasible for a substantial subset of fatal crashes, but it must be designed as probabilistic record linkage[fn:record-linkage] with explicit abstention[fn:abstention], not as a search-and-name scraper.

The national backbone should be the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration Fatality Analysis Reporting System. FARS is a census of qualifying fatal motor-vehicle crashes and includes crash, vehicle, person, driver, roadway, location, alcohol, speed, injury, and related variables. Its public data intentionally excludes personal names and similar direct identifiers.

The missing name can often be recovered from public sources because a FARS crash produces a distinctive fingerprint: date, approximate time, state, county, coordinates, road, vehicle combination, collision type, fatality count, victim ages, measured blood-alcohol concentration, speed, and case progression. Unusual combinations can uniquely identify a public police release, prosecutor statement, court record, or local-news report.

The system must prefer precision over coverage. A correct unresolved result is better than a plausible but wrong name.

* Known-Case Feasibility Test

The existing Kearns, Utah seed provides a deterministic fixture.

Without placing the driver's name in the query, the following characteristics identify the public case:

- January 14, 2024;
- Kearns, Utah;
- Kia Sorento and Volkswagen Jetta;
- head-on collision;
- estimated 77 miles per hour in a 35-mile-per-hour zone;
- reported blood-alcohol concentration of 0.241;
- five-year-old fatal victim.

Public reporting then identifies Charmayne Huerta Guzman and follows the case from arrest and upgraded charges through guilty plea and sentencing.

This proves that at least some FARS-like rows contain enough quasi-identifiers[fn:quasi-identifier] to locate a named public case. It does not prove that all crashes can be named or that one matching article is sufficient verification.

* National Input Landscape

** Fatality Analysis Reporting System

FARS is the primary source because it covers every qualifying fatal crash nationwide. Relevant tables and variables include:

- crash identifiers and event dates;
- state, county, city, latitude, and longitude;
- road and intersection characteristics;
- vehicle make, model, model year, and body type;
- driver and occupant roles;
- fatality and injury counts;
- police-reported alcohol involvement;
- alcohol test status and measured results;
- speeding and traffic-law variables;
- driver license status and prior driving-while-intoxicated convictions.

The current NHTSA Crash API[fn:api] exposes case lists, case details, vehicle, person, and location queries, code definitions, and raw dataset access for modern FARS releases.

FARS must remain the immutable anonymous record. A later named identity is a separate StarIntel person linked by a reviewed resolution claim.

** Crash Report Sampling System

The Crash Report Sampling System[fn:crss] is useful for method development on nonfatal police-reported crashes, but it is a statistical sample and removes direct personal identifiers. It should not be mixed with the FARS census without preserving source and sampling semantics.

** National Driver Register

The National Driver Register and Problem Driver Pointer System contain direct driver identity and serious license-action pointers, but they are restricted systems, not public bulk identity feeds. They are outside this design.

** FBI arrest data

Federal Bureau of Investigation Crime Data Explorer and National Incident-Based Reporting System data can provide impaired-driving arrest counts and demographic distributions. Public releases generally do not supply names and therefore provide validation and enforcement context rather than direct identity resolution.

* Public Source Landscape

** Tier 1: authoritative incident and legal sources

- state police and highway patrol releases;
- county sheriff and municipal police releases;
- district attorney and prosecutor releases;
- state court docket pages and filed documents;
- published appellate opinions;
- official medical-examiner or coroner releases where public.

These sources receive the highest authority weight but still require crash-role matching. A prosecutor page naming a victim, witness, or other driver must not be mistaken for the impaired driver.

** Tier 2: established local reporting

Local newspapers and television stations frequently quote probable-cause affidavits, charging documents, prosecutors, and police. They are essential because state trial-court access is fragmented and some official pages disappear.

A single local report may create a candidate, but identity verification should normally require an official source or an independent second report matching distinctive crash facts.

** Tier 3: discovery and archive systems

- Brave Search for current web and news discovery;
- GDELT for historical story discovery and date-bounded searches;
- Internet Archive Wayback CDX for recovering known dead pages;
- Common Crawl indexes for locating older captures after a URL or domain is known.

Archives recover evidence; they do not upgrade the authority of the original publisher.

** Tier 4: open-data catalogs

- Data.gov Catalog API;
- ArcGIS Hub Search;
- Socrata Discovery API;
- CKAN package search;
- Police Data Accessibility Project.

These systems help discover state and local crash, police, court, and public-safety datasets. They require a source registry because schemas and publication practices differ by jurisdiction.

** Tier 5: public-record request escalation

MuckRock can help locate earlier requests, agencies, released files, and request workflows. Automatic research may draft an escalation plan, but it must not submit a public-record request without explicit operator authorization.

* Search Strategy

The resolver should create a portfolio of small, high-information queries instead of one long query whose terms may be ignored.

** Date and place family

- exact date plus city and fatal crash;
- month, county, and impaired-driving terms;
- date range plus intersection or road name;
- state plus victim age and fatality count.

** Vehicle family

- exact make/model pair;
- uncommon model plus city and crash type;
- vehicle year, body type, and county;
- first vehicle plus second vehicle plus fatality.

** Numeric family

- quoted measured blood-alcohol concentration;
- estimated speed and speed limit;
- victim age;
- unusual occupant or fatality count.

Measured values are high-information features. Imputed blood-alcohol values must not be searched as if they were measured facts.

** Official-domain family

- site-restricted police, sheriff, prosecutor, court, and government queries;
- case-lifecycle terms such as charged, indicted, pleaded, convicted, and sentenced;
- candidate-name follow-up only after the candidate was discovered from a crash query.

** Time-window family

- crash date plus or minus seven days for initial reports;
- seven to forty-five days for toxicology and upgraded charges;
- two to twelve months for indictment, plea, or trial;
- one to three years for sentencing and appellate decisions.

* Three-Proof Resolution Model

** Proof 1: event match

Determine whether a candidate source describes the same physical crash. Compare:

- event date and time;
- distance between coordinates or matching road/intersection;
- county and city;
- vehicle combination;
- collision type;
- fatality and injury profile;
- victim ages;
- speed and speed limit;
- measured alcohol result.

** Proof 2: driver-role match

Determine whether the named person is the specific driver represented by the anonymous FARS person record. Extract explicit roles such as:

- suspected impaired driver;
- other driver;
- passenger;
- pedestrian;
- victim;
- witness;
- vehicle owner;
- police officer;
- prosecutor;
- judge.

A person mentioned near the word "driver" is not sufficient. The source must connect the person to the relevant vehicle or conduct.

** Proof 3: identity and legal-status match

Normalize names, aliases, age-at-event, residence, court identifiers, and source language. Preserve the status actually supported:

- suspected;
- arrested;
- charged;
- indicted;
- pleaded guilty;
- convicted;
- sentenced;
- acquitted;
- dismissed;
- deceased before prosecution.

Do not publish a person as convicted when the available evidence only supports an arrest.

* Probabilistic Linkage

The scoring method should follow the Fellegi-Sunter model[fn:fellegi-sunter] or an equivalent calibrated log-likelihood approach.

For each comparison feature, estimate:

- the probability of agreement when records refer to the same crash;
- the probability of agreement when they refer to different crashes;
- the information contribution of agreement, disagreement, or missing data.

Rare agreements, such as the same unusual vehicle pair and exact measured blood-alcohol result, receive more weight than common agreements such as the same state.

Candidate generation uses blocking[fn:blocking] to avoid comparing every crash to every article. Initial blocks may include:

- state and event-date window;
- county and month;
- geospatial radius and date;
- vehicle make/model and year;
- victim age and event month.

The final score must be calibrated on labeled examples. Handwritten weights are acceptable only for the first deterministic prototype and must be replaced or justified by benchmark evidence.

* Resolution States

- =unresolved=: no reliable candidate;
- =candidates-found=: one or more names discovered;
- =event-matched=: source and FARS crash likely describe the same event;
- =driver-role-matched=: candidate is explicitly the relevant driver;
- =identity-probable=: evidence is strong but below publication threshold;
- =identity-verified=: publication gate requirements met;
- =identity-rejected=: candidate contradicted or mismatched;
- =superseded=: a later resolution replaces the current one;
- =retracted=: previously published resolution withdrawn.

Every transition records evidence identifiers, actor, configuration hash, timestamp, and reason.

* Verification Policy

A candidate may reach =identity-verified= through either path:

1. one authoritative police, prosecutor, or court source explicitly identifies the relevant driver and matches the date, jurisdiction, and multiple crash characteristics; or
2. two independent reputable sources identify the same driver role and match several distinctive crash attributes, with at least one source citing official records.

A single search-result snippet is never verification. A single article without direct official attribution remains =identity-probable= unless the article itself publishes and accurately locates a court document.

The first benchmark target is at least 99 percent precision, with a preferred target of 99.5 percent, for automatically published identities. Coverage is secondary.

* Alcohol-Data Semantics

The actor pipeline must distinguish:

- measured blood-alcohol concentration;
- reported positive/negative test without a numeric result;
- police-reported alcohol involvement;
- imputed blood-alcohol values;
- unknown or not reported.

The ten FARS imputation values represent multiple statistical draws. They must not be averaged into a fake measured result and must not be used as an exact web-search key. Imputed values may support population analysis, not direct identity proof.

* Source Independence

Two URLs are not automatically two independent sources.

The resolver should detect:

- syndicated copies;
- television affiliates repeating the same wire story;
- articles quoting the same police release;
- prosecutor releases copied into news stories;
- updates that replace earlier versions;
- pages sharing identical or near-identical text.

Independent corroboration requires distinct reporting or direct access to separate underlying records. Shared origin should be recorded in source lineage.

* Contradiction Handling

Contradictions stop automatic publication when they concern:

- event date beyond an allowed reporting discrepancy;
- location or county;
- vehicle assignment;
- driver role;
- victim count or identity;
- measured alcohol result;
- candidate age;
- case number;
- dismissal, acquittal, or later correction.

Minor spelling variants and rounded speeds may be normalized. Material conflicts remain visible and lower the resolution state.

* Publication and Retraction

The named person record and the FARS anonymous person remain separate.

The writer emits:

- a named =person= document;
- an entity-resolution =claim=;
- a =resolved_as= relation from anonymous person to named person;
- source and evidence records;
- an independent-review result;
- a resolution-run artifact containing scores and configuration.

A retraction creates a new revision that marks the resolution retracted and links the former claim through lineage. Historical evidence is preserved rather than silently deleted.

* Benchmark Design

Use at least 100 labeled FARS cases, expanding to 200 before national-scale execution.

Strata:

- child fatalities;
- multiple fatalities;
- ordinary two-vehicle crashes;
- single-vehicle crashes;
- rural crashes;
- urban crashes;
- measured blood-alcohol cases;
- alcohol-imputed or unknown cases;
- cases with prosecution;
- cases where the relevant driver died and no prosecution occurred;
- diverse states and years.

Fixture 001 is the Kearns, Utah case. Include negative controls with similar dates, vehicle types, and counties to test false matches.

Metrics:

- candidate discovery rate;
- event-match precision and recall;
- driver-role precision and recall;
- verified-name precision;
- verified-name recall;
- abstention rate;
- calibration error;
- source independence;
- contradictory-evidence capture;
- requests, elapsed time, bytes, and cost per verified identity;
- archive-recovery yield;
- reviewer disagreement;
- retraction rate after later case updates.

No rollout should claim national identity coverage until the benchmark reports false positives and confidence calibration.

* Recommended Actor Boundary

The complete actor design is specified in =DRUNK-DRIVER-RESOLUTION-001=. The research supports a staged pipeline with:

- FARS ingestion and crash fingerprinting;
- jurisdiction and source-registry resolution;
- bounded search and archive discovery;
- page capture and structured extraction;
- candidate generation and probabilistic linkage;
- official corroboration and independent review;
- publication, lifecycle monitoring, and benchmark calibration.

The Resolution Run Supervisor owns budgets, cancellation, terminal state, and retry policy. Search and extraction actors never write canonical identities directly.

* Negative Findings

- No public national bulk source of named impaired drivers was found.
- FARS intentionally omits personal names.
- The National Driver Register cannot be used as a public identity feed.
- CourtListener does not provide complete state trial-court coverage.
- State and local court access remains fragmented.
- Search-engine rank is not evidence of identity.
- Two copied articles do not constitute independent corroboration.
- Imputed blood-alcohol values cannot identify an individual crash as if they were measured.
- A model confidence score without calibrated benchmark evidence is not sufficient for publication.
- Full automatic naming of every FARS crash is neither realistic nor desirable.

* Footnotes and Glossary

[fn:fars] Fatality Analysis Reporting System, or FARS: A United States national census of qualifying fatal motor-vehicle crashes assembled by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration from state records.

[fn:record-linkage] Record linkage: A statistical process for deciding whether records from different systems refer to the same real-world event or entity.

[fn:abstention] Abstention: A deliberate unresolved result used when the available evidence is not strong enough to make a safe automatic decision.

[fn:quasi-identifier] Quasi-identifier: A fact that is not a direct name or identifier but can become distinctive when combined with other facts, such as date, county, vehicle model, victim age, and speed.

[fn:api] Application Programming Interface, or API: A documented machine-to-machine interface used to request structured data or actions.

[fn:crss] Crash Report Sampling System, or CRSS: A nationally representative sample of police-reported motor-vehicle crashes used to estimate nonfatal and fatal crash patterns.

[fn:fellegi-sunter] Fellegi-Sunter model: A statistical record-linkage method that compares how likely each field agreement is among true matches versus nonmatches and combines those values into a decision score.

[fn:blocking] Blocking: A candidate-generation technique that restricts comparisons to records sharing broad features, such as state and date, so the system does not compare every possible pair.
