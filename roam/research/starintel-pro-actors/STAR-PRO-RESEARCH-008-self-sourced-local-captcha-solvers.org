#+title: STAR-PRO-RESEARCH-008 Self-Sourced Local CAPTCHA Solver Research
#+description: Research for replacing routine paid CAPTCHA solving with a local-first, self-hosted actor fabric using synthetic corpora, open benchmarks, OCR, speech recognition, vision-language models, classical computer vision, abstention, and measured fallback.
#+status: REVIEW
#+filetags: :starintel:research:starintel-pro-actors:captcha:local:ml:ocr:vision:audio:synthetic-data:actors:uml:

* Scope

Research whether StarIntel can eliminate or sharply reduce recurring CAPTCHA-provider spend by operating its own solver capabilities.

The target is not one universal model. The target is a local-first portfolio of exact capabilities registered behind the existing authorized CAPTCHA broker. Each solver must advertise only the challenge families, result kinds, interaction depth, and confidence ranges it has actually demonstrated.

This work remains restricted to operator-owned systems, accessibility workflows, quality assurance, open research benchmarks, synthetic environments, and explicitly authorized automation. It does not approve a public solve relay or uncontrolled acquisition of proprietary/live challenge material.

* Executive Finding

Yes: StarIntel can reasonably self-host a useful CAPTCHA-solving tier and make paid providers a non-default fallback.

The strongest evidence is no longer limited to old OCR demonstrations. In May 2026, CaptchaMind reported a training-oriented CAPTCHA benchmark with 16,000 programmatically generated samples across eight task categories and process/region annotations. Its trained solver reported 82.9% average success on the benchmark and 71.0% on real-world instances. This does not prove universal vendor coverage, but it is direct evidence that synthetic, automatically labeled CAPTCHA training data can produce a practical local solver rather than merely a toy OCR model.

Open CaptchaWorld independently shows why the architecture must be heterogeneous and closed-loop. Its 20 interactive CAPTCHA types include clicking, dragging, counting, matching, sequence, and slider-style tasks. General multimodal browser agents reached at most 40.0% pass@1 in the reported evaluation while humans reached 93.3%. The lesson is not “use a bigger VLM everywhere.” The lesson is to route exact challenge families to specialized perception and interaction capabilities and abstain when confidence or task support is weak.

* Research Questions

1. Which challenge classes are cheap enough to solve locally today?
2. Which classes require learned multi-step interaction rather than static OCR/classification?
3. Can StarIntel generate its own training corpus without purchasing labels or scraping proprietary challenge assets?
4. Which open components are suitable for local inference?
5. How should Common Lisp actor orchestration and ML workers be divided?
6. How should capability registration prevent a weak solver from being treated as universal?
7. What measurements decide whether local solving is actually cheaper and more reliable than an external provider?

* Local Capability Tiers

| Tier | Challenge shape | Candidate local technique | Expected role |
|------+-----------------+---------------------------+---------------|
| 0 | known test fixture / deterministic owned challenge | fixture answer or test-key flow | integration and CI only |
| 1 | distorted text / simple glyph recognition | PaddleOCR and/or Tesseract, image preprocessing, small specialized recognizer | cheapest production local path |
| 1 | speech/audio transcription | faster-whisper / CTranslate2 plus audio cleanup | local accessibility-oriented path |
| 2 | static image selection / semantic matching | OpenCLIP-style image-text scoring, optional specialized detector/classifier | tile and object selection where prompt semantics are stable |
| 2 | simple slider/template/alignment | OpenCV template/feature matching and geometry | deterministic puzzle families with strong visual structure |
| 3 | multi-step visual-spatial interaction | specialized VLM/agent policy trained on synthetic process annotations | harder interactive puzzles |
| 4 | unknown, browser-bound, device-bound, behavioral, or low-confidence | abstain, source fallback, manual review, or explicitly enabled external provider | never silently widen capability |

The broker should rank the local tiers before paid external providers when authorization and exact capability requirements match.

* Why Synthetic Data Is the Core Strategy

CaptchaMind is especially relevant because it demonstrates a programmatic generator approach rather than relying only on manual collection. Its CaptchaBench contains 16,000 generated examples across eight CAPTCHA task categories with region-level and process-level annotations. Those annotations are exactly the kind of supervision StarIntel needs for =selectionSet=, =coordinates=, and =interactionPlan= result families, not just final-answer strings.

Open CaptchaWorld also documents a curation pipeline where CAPTCHA-like tasks are built as open benchmark puzzles rather than scraped from commercial systems. It separates visual task construction, interaction design, reasoning-depth annotation, and final ground truth.

For StarIntel, the preferred corpus order is therefore:

1. programmatically generated synthetic challenges with deterministic ground truth;
2. open research benchmarks with explicit redistribution/license metadata;
3. first-party StarIntel-owned CAPTCHA test environments;
4. operator-supplied labeled fixtures with explicit provenance;
5. live explicitly authorized canaries only for final generalization measurement.

Do not make uncontrolled harvesting of proprietary vendor challenge images the training dependency. Synthetic generation gives exact labels, infinite variation, reproducible seeds, license clarity, and much cheaper iteration.

* Synthetic Corpus Families

The first corpus generator should cover families that can be represented without cloning a vendor implementation:

- distorted text with font, rotation, warp, occlusion, line/noise, background, color, compression, and resolution variation;
- audio digits/words with speaker, speed, noise, overlap, clipping, codec, gain, and silence variation;
- image-grid semantic selection from licensed/open image pools;
- count-the-object tasks;
- icon/reference matching;
- sequence-click tasks;
- slider/alignment puzzles;
- jigsaw/piece-placement tasks;
- rotate-to-upright or orientation tasks;
- simple spatial relation tasks;
- synthetic multi-stage tasks that require state tracking after each action.

The Python =captcha= project is a useful low-complexity generator for text and audio fixtures. Its current documentation exposes =ImageCaptcha= and =AudioCaptcha= and configurable character rotation/warp settings. It is a starting component, not a full modern benchmark.

* OCR Findings

PaddleOCR remains a strong local text-recognition candidate. Current PaddleOCR documentation describes local inference, current 3.x tooling, and PP-OCRv6. It supports CPU inference and dedicated text detection/recognition pipelines. For distorted text CAPTCHAs, StarIntel should benchmark both the general OCR pipeline and a small recognizer fine-tuned only on synthetic CAPTCHA glyphs.

Tesseract remains a second independent engine. It is open source under Apache 2.0 and has mature command-line/API integration and trainable LSTM recognition. Its value here is not that it will solve every distorted CAPTCHA; it provides an inexpensive ensemble member and a baseline that makes regression visible.

Recommended local text path:

1. decode and normalize the image;
2. run a small bounded preprocessing ensemble;
3. ask PaddleOCR and Tesseract independently;
4. normalize candidate strings;
5. return only when confidence/agreement crosses the exact family threshold;
6. otherwise escalate to a specialized model or abstain.

A disagreement is useful signal. Do not hide it by selecting whichever answer is non-empty.

* Audio Findings

=faster-whisper= is a practical local speech-recognition worker because it uses CTranslate2 and documents quantized CPU/GPU inference. Audio CAPTCHA tasks are often short, so the actor should use a bounded decode pipeline rather than a general long-form transcription configuration.

The local audio solver should separate:

- signal cleanup;
- voice activity detection;
- transcription;
- task-specific normalization, such as digit/letter vocabulary;
- confidence and alternate hypotheses;
- final typed =textAnswer=.

Synthetic audio generation should vary voices and noise rather than train on one clean voice pack.

* Vision and Semantic Selection Findings

OpenCLIP is a viable open source component for zero-shot image/text similarity. A tile-selection actor can score each image crop against the task phrase and calibrated negative phrases, then return a =selectionSet= only when the margin is strong enough.

This should not be treated as a universal object detector. For recurring challenge families, a small family-specific classifier or detector can outperform zero-shot scoring and be far cheaper. The model registry should therefore allow a hierarchy:

1. exact specialized model;
2. generic local vision embedding/classifier;
3. local reasoning model;
4. abstain.

ONNX Runtime is useful for normalizing CPU inference for exported small models. Keep framework-specific training dependencies out of the Common Lisp control process.

* Classical Computer Vision Findings

OpenCV still matters. Template matching, feature matching, contour/edge analysis, correlation, and geometry can solve synthetic or owned slider/alignment families deterministically and with very low compute.

This is especially attractive when the challenge is mostly geometric rather than semantic. A 20 ms deterministic solver is preferable to invoking a multi-billion-parameter model for every puzzle.

The actor should produce an exact geometric capability such as =captcha.synthetic.slider.coordinates.v1= instead of registering a generic “puzzle solver.”

* Multi-Step Solver Findings

The newer benchmark literature is explicit that many modern puzzles are sequential. The solver observes a screenshot, chooses an action, receives a changed observation, and repeats. Open CaptchaWorld models this as a partially observable decision process rather than a one-shot classifier.

CaptchaMind pushes this further by training with process supervision. Its reported results suggest that region grounding and intermediate interaction feedback materially improve multi-step performance.

StarIntel should therefore represent hard local puzzle solving as a run actor with state:

- observation history;
- current task instruction;
- candidate regions;
- selected action;
- action confidence;
- step count and deadline;
- verification feedback;
- terminal result or abstention.

The model should never be allowed to emit arbitrary browser automation. It emits one typed bounded action from a family-specific action schema, and the authorized source runtime decides whether to apply it.

* Vendor Test Keys Are for Integration, Not Training

Current vendor documentation provides safe test paths for integration testing:

- hCaptcha documents test key sets that always return predictable verification behavior;
- Cloudflare Turnstile documents dummy sitekeys/secrets for predictable pass/fail automated tests;
- Google reCAPTCHA documents v2 test keys and recommends separate test keys for v3.

These are useful for validating StarIntel broker/source-runtime integration without buying solves. They do not provide the challenge diversity needed for training the local perception models.

Common Lisp remains the policy, orchestration, actor, transport, lifecycle, and authorization layer. Python is the pragmatic ML worker boundary for PaddleOCR, faster-whisper, OpenCLIP, ONNX Runtime, OpenCV, and training. The worker interface should be narrow and typed over the existing actor/RabbitMQ infrastructure rather than introducing another public HTTP service.

* Model Registry Requirements

Every local model/capability should record:

- model ID and content hash;
- implementation/runtime version;
- training code hash;
- training corpus manifest hash;
- exact challenge family/variant;
- result kind and action schema;
- supported dimensions/resolution/input constraints;
- CPU/GPU memory profile;
- p50/p95 latency;
- pass@1 and calibrated confidence curves;
- false-action rate;
- abstention rate;
- benchmark version;
- license and redistribution constraints;
- state: =experimental=, =verified=, =degraded=, =invalidContract=, =disabled=;
- rollback predecessor.

A model file existing on disk does not make it a verified capability.

* Benchmark Policy

Metrics should emphasize accepted outcomes, not model confidence alone:

- pass@1;
- success within allowed steps;
- exact string accuracy for text/audio;
- selection precision/recall and exact-set accuracy;
- coordinate error and click hit rate;
- interaction completion rate;
- downstream acceptance rate on owned/authorized canaries;
- p50/p95 local latency;
- CPU time and memory;
- abstention rate;
- false-action rate;
- cost per accepted solve, including compute amortization.

Promotion requires a frozen benchmark suite plus a held-out renderer/style split. New models run shadow evaluation before becoming broker-eligible.

* Economic Finding

The economics favor local solving when the challenge family recurs. OCR, audio transcription, embedding/classification, and classical CV have near-zero marginal provider cost once the worker is running. Synthetic data also removes labeling expense for many families.

Training a large reasoning model from scratch would not be economical. The practical strategy is:

- use specialized deterministic/small models for common families;
- fine-tune an existing open vision-language model only for the hard tail;
- keep the difficult model out of the hot path unless simpler solvers abstain;
- use manual review or a separately enabled external provider for unsupported tail cases.

The decision metric is not “did local solve something once?” It is cost per accepted result at the required latency and error rate.

* Safety and Provenance Findings

The local approach improves privacy because challenge material need not be disclosed to an external worker/provider. It also creates new risks: training-data provenance, model supply chain, unsafe action generation, artifact tampering, and accidental capability widening.

Required controls:

- target policy is checked before any solve work;
- corpus records retain source class and license;
- live challenge material is ephemeral unless explicit authorized retention exists;
- model artifacts are hash-pinned;
- solver outputs are typed, bounded, and capability-specific;
- action actors cannot invent navigation or arbitrary JavaScript;
- confidence thresholds include abstention;
- downstream acceptance is recorded separately from model success;
- benchmark and model promotion are auditable;
- no public arbitrary-target solver endpoint.

* Recommended Build Sequence

1. Implement =CorpusActor= with deterministic text/audio/image-selection/slider generators and manifest hashes.
2. Implement =BenchmarkActor= and the model registry before training anything.
3. Add text OCR ensemble using PaddleOCR plus Tesseract.
4. Add audio transcription using faster-whisper.
5. Add OpenCV geometric solver capabilities.
6. Add OpenCLIP semantic tile scoring and exact selection-set confidence calibration.
7. Import Open CaptchaWorld as an external benchmark only after license/provenance review.
8. Reproduce a small subset of CaptchaBench-style programmatic task generation with region and process annotations.
9. Add a specialized multi-step local VLM policy only for benchmark families where simpler solvers fail.
10. Put the local router ahead of paid providers; paid provider capabilities remain disabled by default unless the operator explicitly enables them.

* Research Gaps

- Confirm the current public code/data license and reproducibility state of CaptchaMind/CaptchaBench before importing any artifact.
- Benchmark PaddleOCR, Tesseract, faster-whisper, OpenCLIP, and OpenCV on the actual StarIntel synthetic corpus rather than extrapolating from general benchmarks.
- Select one open VLM family for multi-step training based on license, CPU/GPU feasibility, export path, and region-grounding quality.
- Determine whether local model inference should be long-lived Python workers or supervised per-model subprocesses.
- Define exact RabbitMQ message size limits so images/audio travel by artifact reference rather than oversized messages.
- Build a license matrix for every model weight and imported benchmark.
- Define retained-vs-ephemeral policy for authorized real-world canary captures.
- Quantify compute cost per accepted result before retiring any external fallback entirely.

* Footnotes and Glossary

- *Self-sourced solver* — A local StarIntel capability whose inference and primary training/benchmark pipeline do not depend on per-solve external CAPTCHA providers.
- *Synthetic corpus* — Programmatically generated challenge instances with deterministic ground truth, provenance, and replay metadata.
- *Abstention* — An explicit local solver result indicating insufficient confidence or unsupported capability; it is preferable to a guessed action.
- *Process supervision* — Training/evaluation data describing intermediate regions or actions rather than only the final success/failure label.
