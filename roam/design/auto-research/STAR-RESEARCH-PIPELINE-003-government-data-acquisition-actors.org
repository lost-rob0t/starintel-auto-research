* REVIEW Objective

Define the actors that execute the government-data acquisition strategy from =STAR-RESEARCH-003=.

The design must ingest authoritative government registries, resolve identities, discover official domains and delegated vendor systems, profile sources, dispatch bounded acquisition jobs, preserve raw artifacts, normalize records, validate provenance, schedule refreshes, and report coverage gaps.

The design must scale across the 91,438 local governments reported in the 2025 Census Government Organization series without creating one permanently running actor per government.

* REVIEW Scope

- one government-data domain gserver
- Common Lisp and Sento actor ownership
- root and per-run supervision trees
- typed ingress and control operations
- durable registry, source-profile, and coverage state

- identity resolution and conflict review
- official-domain discovery and verification

** Deferred

* REVIEW Architecture Decision

Create one logical government-data domain gserver under the StarIntel actor system:

#+begin_example
/user/domains/government-data
#+end_example

The domain owns control and workflow actors. CPU-heavy parsers, browser workers, OCR, archive extraction, and adapters with risky dependency trees may run in supervised subprocesses behind the same message and result contracts.

Government units are data records and job keys. They are not permanent actor identities.

Source adapters are reusable worker types. A bounded job identifies the government unit, source profile, source cursor, limits, and expected data family.

ADARD remains the research control plane when government acquisition is invoked as a deep-research capability. The government-data gserver owns acquisition execution and durable acquisition state, but it may not promote research conclusions or mutate ADARD candidate state directly.

* REVIEW Invariants

1. No actor is created permanently for each government unit.
2. Canonical government identity is written only through the Government Identity Authority.
3. Canonical source profiles are written only through the Source Profile Authority.
4. Coverage state is append-only and written only through the Coverage Ledger.
5. Adapter workers do not write canonical documents directly.
6. A normalized record is not accepted before its raw artifact or explicit non-retained-source record exists.
7. Every normalized record resolves to a source profile, retrieval event, source locator, and content hash.
8. Every job declares fixed request, byte, object, time, depth, and concurrency bounds.
9. Network capabilities are granted by manifest and source scope, never inferred from retrieved content.
10. The Rate and Lease Authority owns per-host, per-vendor, and per-key concurrency.
11. A job is dispatched at least once and may be replayed; no component claims exactly-once delivery.
12. Idempotency keys prevent a replay from duplicating canonical state.
13. Mailbox contents are not durable job storage.
14. A process crash after raw-artifact storage but before normalization must be recoverable.
15. Cancellation uses one stable cancel identifier through every child job.
16. A worker may return partial data only with declared truncation and checkpoint state.
17. Search results create candidates, not verified official-source links.
18. Restricted federal APIs cannot be used to build a public dataset.
19. Browser and OCR workers cannot access canonical ledgers directly.
20. Records-request messages remain drafts until an operator explicitly authorizes sending.
21. Actor restart never silently resets rate-limit, cursor, or coverage state.
22. Source retirement and government dissolution create tombstones instead of deletions.
23. One malformed source cannot stop unrelated jurisdictions or adapters.
24. A polished report cannot substitute for acquisition evidence.
25. The design does not enter the implementation slot until reviewed and approved.

* REVIEW Root Supervision

The Government Data Domain Supervisor uses =:rest-for-one= for startup-ordered authorities:

1. control and ingress
2. registry authorities
3. discovery authorities
4. acquisition authorities
5. evidence authorities
6. coverage authorities
7. external worker bridges

If an earlier authority fails during initialization, later components restart because their handles and readiness assumptions may be invalid.

Normal child failures use one-for-one restart below each subtree.

Restart policy:

- maximum five restarts per child in sixty seconds
- exponential delay with jitter after the first restart
- permanent stop and domain degradation after budget exhaustion
- no automatic restart for configuration-invalid or schema-unsupported failures
- replay from durable state after authority restart
- child generation included in every health and event record

The root reports =:ready= only when identity, source-profile, adapter-registry, raw-artifact, rate, and coverage authorities have replayed their durable state.

* REVIEW Per-Run Supervision

Each acquisition run creates one temporary coordinator subtree:

#+begin_example
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>/registry
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>/discovery
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>/acquisition
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>/normalization
/user/domains/government-data/runs/<run-id>/audit
#+end_example

The run coordinator owns orchestration but not canonical state.

Per-run strategy is =:one-for-all= only for tightly coupled ephemeral branch actors whose checkpoints cannot be advanced independently. Adapter jobs themselves remain isolated one-for-one children or routed work items.

A run terminates after:

- all requested work reaches a terminal state
- cancellation completes
- the run deadline expires
- its retry budget is exhausted
- an authority rejects the run configuration

Run termination never stops domain authorities or unrelated runs.

* REVIEW Durable Document Types

The actor design requires the following durable document families. Exact StarIntel 0.8 field mapping remains a schema implementation task.

** Government Unit

Represents an independent state or local government, tribal or territorial government, or federal organization at an explicitly declared hierarchy level.

Required concepts:

- stable StarIntel identifier
- canonical name
- government level and type
- parent and dependency relations
- authoritative identifiers
- aliases
- validity interval
- active status
- source evidence
- registry vintage

** Government Component

Represents a department, bureau, office, board, commission, court, authority, school system, pension system, or other component that must not be confused with an independent government.

** Government Identifier

Represents one namespace-specific identifier and its validity, issuer, confidence, and evidence.

** Official Domain

Represents a domain candidate, verification state, delegated role, redirects, organization claim, and government link.

** Government Source Profile

Represents one catalog, API, feed, vendor tenant, document repository, official page family, or public portal.

Required concepts:

- government or component link
- source type
- platform and version
- canonical base URL
- delegated-vendor evidence
- data families
- supported methods
- pagination
- change semantics
- authentication
- rate limits
- acquisition adapter
- parser
- raw-retention policy
- verification state
- validity interval
- last discovery and reconciliation

** Government Acquisition Event

Represents one immutable retrieval, checkpoint, parse, normalization, validation, or terminal event.

** Government Raw Artifact

Represents retained bytes or an explicit non-retained observation with content hash, locator, timestamps, media type, and storage reference.

** Government Coverage Observation

Represents one append-only statement about unit, source, family, freshness, history, success, or gap.

** Government Gap

Represents a missing, blocked, ambiguous, unpublished, historical, or records-request-only data family.

* REVIEW State Authorities

** Government Identity Authority

Owns canonical government units, components, identifiers, aliases, parent relationships, validity intervals, and tombstones.

Accepts proposals from registry parsers and resolvers.

It must:

- serialize writes for one canonical identity key
- compare source authority and vintage
- preserve contradictory candidates
- create merge and split review tasks
- reject destructive overwrite
- emit identity-change events
- maintain idempotency records
- replay from the identity ledger

It does not perform network retrieval.

** Source Profile Authority

Owns source-profile lifecycle.

States:

#+begin_example
:candidate
:verifying
:verified
:degraded
:authentication-required
:blocked
:migrated
:retired
:unsupported
:rejected
#+end_example

Only verified or explicitly operator-approved profiles may schedule broad acquisition.

A profile change creates a new version. The previous version remains addressable for replay.

** Adapter Registry

Owns the mapping from source capability and platform fingerprint to a versioned adapter manifest.

It rejects:

- duplicate adapter IDs
- mutable version references
- missing schemas
- undeclared network methods
- missing limits
- unsupported result protocols
- adapters that can write canonical state
- adapters that request unrestricted credentials
- adapter fallbacks not declared in the profile

** Rate and Lease Authority

Owns network concurrency and retry timing.

Lease keys may include:

- hostname
- vendor
- API key
- tenant
- adapter
- government unit
- source profile

It maintains:

- active lease count
- token bucket or fixed-window state
- Retry-After deadlines
- observed 429 and 503 history
- circuit-breaker state
- crawl-delay policy when applicable
- host health
- per-run quota
- lease generation

Workers cannot issue a network request before obtaining a lease.

A worker crash expires its lease by deadline or watcher notification.

** Raw Artifact Authority

Owns artifact metadata commits and storage references.

The byte store may be CouchDB attachments, object storage, WARC storage, or another content-addressed service. The actor does not hold large artifacts in its mailbox.

Commit sequence:

1. reserve artifact identity
2. stream or hand off bytes outside actor mailbox
3. verify byte count and hash
4. atomically commit artifact metadata
5. emit =:artifact-stored=
6. release normalization eligibility

Incomplete reservations are garbage-collected only after recovery checks.

** Coverage Ledger

Owns append-only coverage observations and schedule-driving state.

It must not overwrite historical observations. Materialized current coverage is derived from replay.

* REVIEW Actor Specifications

** Government Data Domain Supervisor

Role:

- start subtrees in dependency order
- enforce restart budgets
- expose domain health
- coordinate drain and shutdown
- publish manifest and capability snapshots

Mailbox:

- capacity 256
- overload =:reply-busy=
- control dispatcher
- no blocking calls without deadlines

Failure policy:

- =:escalate=
- =:rest-for-one= at root

** Government Data Ingress Actor

Role:

- validate public commands
- authenticate caller capability
- resolve domain handle
- clamp limits
- reject unsupported protocol versions
- create or route a run coordinator

It never performs retrieval or parsing.

Mailbox:

- capacity 1,024
- overload =:reply-busy=
- duplicate command IDs return prior acceptance state

** Acquisition Run Coordinator

Role:

- expand one accepted run into bounded registry, discovery, acquisition, normalization, or audit commands
- preserve run budget
- collect terminal results
- coordinate cancellation
- report progress

State is checkpointed in an append-only run ledger. The coordinator can be recreated from the ledger.

Routing key:

- run ID

** Registry Release Actor

Role:

- retrieve or accept one authoritative registry release
- store raw release artifact
- parse release metadata
- emit identity proposals and release reconciliation counts

Initial profiles:

- Census Government Units Listing
- CISA .gov domains and zone file
- SAM public Federal Hierarchy
- FOIA.gov agency components
- NCES Common Core of Data
- other approved registry releases

Registry workers never commit identity directly.

** Government Identity Resolver

Role:

- compare proposed units and components
- score exact identifiers, names, addresses, hierarchy, geography, domains, and validity
- emit match, new-entity, merge-review, split-review, or unresolved proposals

It may use Prolog through a declared pure capability for deterministic relationship rules.

It cannot silently merge records based only on name similarity.

Pool routing:

- stable candidate key or authoritative identifier namespace

** Domain Registry Actor

Role:

- ingest domain registries
- normalize domains
- detect additions, removals, DNS changes, and ownership metadata changes
- emit domain-to-government candidates

It does not assert official linkage by itself.

** Official Domain Resolver

Role:

- follow bounded redirects
- inspect official claims, certificates, metadata, contact links, and cross-links
- compare registry identity and government identity
- classify primary, component, service, campaign, retired, or unrelated domain
- emit evidence-backed verification proposals

Capabilities:

- DNS
- TLS observation
- HTTP HEAD and bounded GET
- HTML metadata extraction
- no browser by default

** Source Profile Actor

Role:

- inventory catalogs, APIs, feeds, meeting systems, code systems, procurement portals, document collections, and data-family landing pages
- store candidate evidence
- request platform fingerprints
- propose source-profile versions

It must record negative discovery and expected-family gaps.

** Platform Fingerprint Actor

Role:

- test declared signatures
- identify platform, tenant, client, service IDs, versions, and public capabilities
- choose candidate adapters
- retain evidence for each conclusion

Initial fingerprints:

- ArcGIS
- Socrata or Tyler Data & Insights
- CKAN
- Huwise or OpenDataSoft
- Granicus Legistar
- eCode360
- Accela
- GeoServer and OGC
- DCAT-US
- generic OpenAPI
- RSS, Atom, and iCalendar
- generic HTML and file repository

A fingerprint result includes confidence and contradictions. Only Source Profile Authority changes the canonical profile.

** Acquisition Dispatcher

Role:

- validate source profile and adapter selection
- obtain rate lease
- create bounded adapter attempt
- route output to raw-artifact authority
- checkpoint cursors
- enforce cancellation
- return terminal normalized adapter result

Routing key:

- source-profile ID for ordered cursors
- host or vendor key for lease coordination

It does not parse domain records itself.

** Platform Adapter Actor

A platform adapter is a stateless or checkpoint-bounded worker behind the dispatcher.

Every adapter:

- accepts one immutable command
- reads one source profile version
- uses only declared methods and credentials
- obtains leases for requests
- emits events
- stores raw artifacts through the authority
- returns checkpoints and counts
- never writes canonical identities, profiles, or coverage
- terminates after one attempt

Initial adapter families:

- DCAT-US catalog
- Data.gov Catalog API
- ArcGIS item and service
- Socrata SODA
- CKAN catalog and DataStore
- Huwise or OpenDataSoft Explore
- Legistar
- eCode360
- Accela public API
- OGC WFS, WMS, WMTS, and feature services
- generic RSS, Atom, and iCalendar
- generic file and document
- generic structured HTML
- federal system APIs
- browser-worker adapter
- deterministic fake adapter

** Federal System Adapter Actor

Federal adapters remain normal platform adapters but declare the federal source contract.

Initial profiles:

- USAspending
- SAM public APIs
- Federal Audit Clearinghouse
- Grants.gov or Simpler.Grants.gov
- Congress.gov
- GovInfo API and bulk repository
- Federal Register
- Regulations.gov
- OpenFEC
- FOIA.gov
- National Archives Catalog and approved bulk path
- OpenFEMA
- Treasury Fiscal Data

A federal adapter must distinguish metadata, informational renditions, and official legal publications.

** Generic Document Actor

Role:

- acquire a bounded document or archive
- detect type
- extract text and structural metadata using a declared parser
- preserve original artifact
- reject active content or unsafe archives
- route image-only pages to an OCR request only when authorized

This actor must not contain platform-specific network discovery.

** Browser Worker Host

Role:

- launch one isolated browser worker for a verified source profile requiring JavaScript
- expose only declared fetch, page-read, download, and event capabilities
- enforce destination allowlist
- capture network and document artifacts
- terminate on deadline or cancellation

The browser worker cannot access:

- identity ledger
- source-profile ledger
- coverage ledger
- arbitrary credentials
- unrelated tabs or origins
- filesystem outside its sandbox
- another worker's memory

** Normalization Dispatcher

Role:

- choose a parser and normalizer from source profile and artifact type
- deduplicate normalization work by artifact hash and parser version
- route to CPU-bound workers
- collect normalized proposals
- request provenance validation

Routing key:

- artifact hash and normalizer version

** Government Normalizer Actor

Role:

- transform platform-specific records into StarIntel document proposals
- preserve source IDs and source-specific fields
- emit relationship proposals
- mark uncertainty and parse warnings
- produce deterministic output for identical inputs

It has no network capability.

Normalizer families should correspond to data families, not only platforms:

- organization and official
- meeting and legislative action
- law and code
- finance and procurement
- GIS and infrastructure
- permit and license
- election
- education
- public safety and court
- notice and records-request metadata

** Provenance Validator

Role:

- verify source-profile version
- resolve artifact and locator
- verify content hashes and parser version
- ensure required record fields
- enforce bounds and truncation declarations
- reject unresolved citations or source IDs
- emit commit-ready proposals

Only validated proposals may be sent to canonical document persistence and coverage observation.

** Coverage Auditor Actor

Role:

- compare canonical units with source profiles and expected data families
- inspect freshness, failures, historical depth, and provenance
- create reconciliation commands
- generate explicit gaps
- calculate coverage metrics by government type and state

Audits must not count a large city's many datasets as coverage for governments with no source profile.

** Refresh Scheduler

Role:

- derive due jobs from coverage and source-profile policy
- issue bounded commands
- enforce per-source and per-run quotas
- honor backoff and circuit breakers
- schedule full reconciliation in addition to incremental updates

The scheduler persists intent before dispatch and completion after result. Mailbox loss cannot erase a due job.

** Gap and Records Request Actor

Role:

- materialize unresolved coverage gaps
- prepare a records-request candidate using public instructions
- track request status and produced artifacts
- require explicit operator authorization before sending

Default capabilities do not include email, form submission, payment, or legal acceptance.

* REVIEW Capability Model

Capabilities are explicit values passed by the host, not ambient global functions.

Initial capability classes:

| Capability | Permitted actors |
|------------+------------------|
| registry artifact read | registry release workers |
| canonical identity propose | resolver and provenance validator |
| canonical identity commit | Government Identity Authority only |
| source-profile propose | discovery and fingerprint actors |
| source-profile commit | Source Profile Authority only |
| DNS and TLS observe | domain resolver and profiler |
| bounded HTTP GET or HEAD | discovery and network adapters |
| API credential reference | specific adapter and source profile |
| raw artifact reserve and commit | dispatcher and Raw Artifact Authority |
| object-store write | Raw Artifact Authority or isolated streamer |
| local parse | normalizers and document workers |
| browser session | Browser Worker Host only |
| OCR | isolated OCR adapter only |
| coverage append | Provenance Validator, Auditor, Scheduler result path |
| records-request draft | Gap actor |
| records-request send | disabled by default; operator-gated separate capability |

Retrieved HTML, JSON, JavaScript, documents, and model output cannot add capabilities.

* REVIEW Mailbox and Overload Policy

Control and authority messages must never be dropped.

| Actor class | Capacity | Overload |
|-------------+----------+----------|
| root and supervisors | 256 | =:reply-busy= |
| ingress | 1,024 | =:reply-busy= |
| state authorities | 1,024 | =:reply-busy= |
| run coordinators | 2,048 | =:reply-busy= |
| discovery pools | 512 per routee | =:reply-busy= |
| acquisition adapters | 128 per routee | =:reply-busy= |
| normalizers | 128 per routee | =:reply-busy= |
| metrics-only subscribers | bounded | =:drop-old= permitted |
| event archive writer | 4,096 | =:reply-busy= and durable spool |

A busy result returns the retry-after suggestion and does not consume the command idempotency key as completed.

Blocking enqueue without a deadline is forbidden.

Shutdown:

- authorities use =:deadline= drain
- adapter attempts receive cancellation and a grace period
- large downloads are aborted and checkpointed
- metrics subscribers may discard nonessential buffered samples
- uncommitted canonical proposals remain in durable run state

* REVIEW Routing

Use message-aware keyed routing.

| Work | Key |
|------|-----|
| identity proposals | authoritative ID namespace and value |
| source-profile changes | source-profile ID |
| domain verification | normalized domain |
| platform fingerprinting | source-profile ID |
| acquisition | source-profile ID and cursor partition |
| rate leases | host, vendor, key, and tenant |
| raw artifacts | artifact reservation ID |
| normalization | artifact hash and parser version |
| coverage observations | government ID and data family |
| schedules | source-profile ID |

Keyed routing preserves serialization where required while permitting unrelated units and sources to run concurrently.

* REVIEW Job Durability and Delivery

Dispatch is at least once.

A durable job record moves through:

#+begin_example
:planned
:queued
:accepted
:running
:checkpointed
:normalizing
:validating
:completed
:partial
:failed
:cancelled
:gap
#+end_example

Transitions are append-only.

A dispatcher crash may cause a replay. Every side effect therefore uses:

- command ID
- idempotency key
- source-profile version
- cursor or page identity
- artifact content hash
- parser version
- canonical proposal hash

A worker acknowledgment is not completion. Completion requires a terminal result accepted by the run coordinator.

* REVIEW Checkpoint Contract

A checkpoint is immutable. Resume creates a new attempt referencing the prior checkpoint.

Adapters must not encode opaque in-memory object references in checkpoints.

* REVIEW Rate and Retry Policy

Retry classes:

| Failure | Default |
|---------+---------|
| connection reset or transient DNS | exponential retry |
| HTTP 408, 425, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504 | honor Retry-After then bounded retry |
| permanent 400 or schema rejection | fail without blind retry |
| 401 or 403 | mark authentication or access gap; no credential guessing |
| 404 on previously valid source | verify migration before retirement |
| checksum mismatch | retry once, then quarantine |
| parser failure | retain artifact and route to parser review |
| rate lease timeout | requeue without issuing request |
| browser crash | retry once in fresh process |
| cancellation | no retry |
| configuration invalid | reject |
| source-profile retired | cancel and reschedule discovery |

Retry state belongs to the durable job and Rate and Lease Authority, not a worker's private memory.

* REVIEW Circuit Breakers

Circuit breaker keys are host or vendor plus source class.

States:

- =:closed=
- =:open=
- =:half-open=

Open conditions may include:

- repeated 429
- repeated 503
- connection failures
- malformed responses across several tenants
- authentication rejection
- explicit maintenance response

One tenant's bad data must not open the entire vendor circuit unless failures show a shared platform outage.

* REVIEW Source Discovery Flow

1. scheduler or operator requests discovery for a government ID
2. run coordinator loads identity and known domains
3. Domain Registry Actor contributes candidates
4. Official Domain Resolver verifies candidates
5. Source Profile Actor crawls only bounded official or delegated paths
6. Platform Fingerprint Pool tests candidate signatures
7. Source Profile Authority commits verified profiles
8. Coverage Ledger records found and missing data families
9. scheduler creates acquisition jobs for eligible profiles

Search-engine or deep-research adapter findings may seed candidates, but they cannot skip domain and source verification.

* REVIEW Acquisition Flow

1. scheduler persists job intent
2. ingress validates command and source-profile version
3. Acquisition Dispatcher resolves adapter
4. Rate and Lease Authority grants lease
5. adapter issues bounded request
6. adapter streams response to artifact storage
7. Raw Artifact Authority commits hash and metadata
8. adapter writes checkpoint
9. Normalization Dispatcher selects normalizer
10. normalizer emits record proposals
11. Provenance Validator validates source and artifact links
12. canonical persistence accepts validated proposals
13. Coverage Ledger appends success, freshness, counts, and history
14. lease closes
15. run coordinator accepts terminal result

If step 7 succeeds and step 10 fails, retry begins from the stored artifact without repeating the network request unless policy requires revalidation.

* REVIEW Source Migration Flow

When a source returns retirement or migration evidence:

1. retain the failed retrieval
2. profile redirect and linked successor
3. create a candidate source-profile version
4. verify delegated ownership
5. mark prior profile =:migrated= only after approval
6. preserve cursor and history mapping when compatible
7. schedule full reconciliation on the new profile
8. create a gap if historical data did not migrate

* REVIEW Normalization and Canonical Writes

Adapters return source-native records and artifacts. Normalizers return proposals.

Canonical writes follow:

#+begin_example
adapter result
  -> artifact commit
  -> deterministic normalizer
  -> provenance validator
  -> document validator
  -> canonical persistence
  -> coverage observation
#+end_example

Identity and source-profile proposals use their dedicated authorities.

Domain records such as meeting, matter, vote, contract, budget, permit, code section, official, and agency may use shared StarIntel persistence after validation.

No adapter receives a CouchDB handle or canonical file path.

* REVIEW Browser and Process Isolation

Use a separate operating-system process when a worker requires:

- full browser
- OCR
- untrusted archive extraction
- heavyweight geospatial conversion
- conflicting dependency versions
- independent memory limit
- stronger crash containment

The process adapter uses the same command, event, checkpoint, and result semantics as local actors.

Standard output contains protocol events only. Standard error becomes a diagnostic artifact.

Cancellation:

1. send cooperative cancel
2. stop accepting new pages
3. flush valid checkpoints
4. close artifact streams
5. terminate process group after grace period
6. emit one terminal cancellation result

* REVIEW Records Request Boundary

The Gap and Records Request Actor may:

- identify a missing expected data family
- locate official request instructions and contacts
- draft a narrowly scoped request
- estimate requested format and date range
- attach evidence of unsuccessful public-source discovery
- track response, appeal, fee, and production state

It may not by default:

- send email
- submit forms
- agree to terms
- accept fees
- disclose operator identity
- appeal or litigate
- repeatedly contact an agency

Those operations require separate operator-authorized capabilities and audit events.

* REVIEW Security Boundary

- all network targets originate from verified source profiles or bounded discovery commands
- redirects are revalidated against destination policy
- private and link-local addresses are blocked unless explicitly configured for a local source
- DNS rebinding is checked
- credentials are opaque scoped references
- secrets do not appear in manifests, events, artifacts, or diagnostics
- TLS validation is enabled by default
- downloaded active content is quarantined
- archive expansion has file-count, path, ratio, and byte limits
- Common Lisp readers bind =*read-eval*= to =nil=
- no retrieved S-expression is evaluated
- HTML and documents are untrusted input
- browser origins are allowlisted
- subprocess argument vectors avoid shell interpolation
- canonical write capabilities are unavailable to adapters
- records-request sending is disabled by default

* REVIEW Acceptance Criteria

The design is ready for approval when review confirms:

- actor ownership is unambiguous
- no actor-per-government architecture exists
- state authorities have one writer
- job delivery and replay are explicit
- adapters cannot write canonical state
- rate authority covers host, vendor, tenant, and credential limits
- raw artifacts precede normalization
- provenance is required for canonical records
- browser and parser isolation is bounded
- cancellation has one terminal result
- records-request sending is operator-gated
- manifests are versioned and validated
- fixtures cover failures and recovery
- the design composes with Sento and ADARD instead of duplicating them

* REVIEW Open Questions

- Which StarIntel 0.8 document types should be canonical versus source-native attachments?
- Which content-addressed raw store should be used first?
- Should the first implementation run fully repository-local or inside a government-data domain gserver?
- Which Prolog rules belong in identity resolution versus deterministic Common Lisp validators?
- What population, geography, and government-type strata define the first pilot?
- Which source families require historical backfill before current synchronization?
- Which rate policies can be shared by vendor and which must be tenant-specific?
- Which normalizers can safely be shared across federal and local sources?
- How should operator review queues appear in Quasar without transferring state ownership to the UI?

* REVIEW Negative Decisions

- Do not create 91,438 permanent actors.
- Do not let each adapter own its scheduler.
- Do not store durable jobs only in mailboxes.
- Do not give adapters direct CouchDB or Org-roam write access.
- Do not route all work through a single generic crawler.
- Do not default to browser automation.
- Do not treat OCR as ordinary parsing.
- Do not guess credentials or bypass access controls.
- Do not treat .gov domains as government identities.
- Do not merge identities from names alone.
- Do not promote search results directly to verified sources.
- Do not retry permanent errors blindly.
- Do not claim exactly-once delivery.
- Do not discard prior source-profile versions.
- Do not erase dissolved governments or retired sources.
- Do not send records requests autonomously by default.

* REVIEW Recommendation

Approve this design as a dependent government-data execution architecture after =STAR-RESEARCH-003= is accepted.

Implementation should begin with protocol schemas, deterministic fake actors, the identity spine, and raw-artifact provenance. Platform adapters should be added only after replay, idempotency, cancellation, and one-writer authority tests pass.

The first national-scale milestone should register all units and profile source coverage. It should not attempt to download every data family before the system can prove what it covered, what failed, and why.
